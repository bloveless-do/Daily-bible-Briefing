**2. Evergrande founder sentenced to life in prison.** A Shenzhen court sentenced Hui Ka Yan, once Asia's richest man, to life in prison for fraud and bribery tied to the collapse of China's Evergrande Group, which left more than $300 billion in liabilities. (Reuters, AP)

## National

**3. Supreme Court allows Trump's mail-voting order to proceed, 6-3.** The Court lifted an injunction that had blocked key parts of Trump's March executive order in 23 states, ruling 6-3 along ideological lines. States must now send DHS a list of adult citizens eligible to vote at least 60 days before a federal election, and the attorney general is directed to prioritize prosecuting officials who issue federal ballots to ineligible voters. The most contested piece, USPS withholding mail ballots from anyone not on a government eligibility list, was not addressed and stays blocked under a separate case. The unsigned majority opinion said the ruling does not mean the order's implementation "will necessarily be lawful." Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented. (Reuters, NPR, SCOTUSblog)

**4. Treasury launches "Operation Economic Outcast" against Iran.** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a new round of secondary sanctions Monday targeting countries and companies that continue trading with Iran, expanding into aviation, digital assets, gold, shipping, and technology. Nearly 60 entities, individuals, and vessels tied to nuclear and missile procurement, cyber operations, and oil smuggling were sanctioned. Bessent said Trump is personally calling world leaders to press them to cut ties with Tehran. (Reuters, NPR)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills sales tax measure certified for the November 3 ballot.** The council voted to place a one-cent local Transactions and Use Tax on the ballot, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for the General Fund. The city faces a projected $4.3 million structural shortfall in FY 2026-27. Groceries, rent, labor, utilities, and prescriptions are exempt. The measure includes annual independent audits and public spending disclosure. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Chino council splits 3-2 to allow murals on building exteriors.** The council voted July 7 to amend the sign code and permit public art on exterior walls after months of debate. Councilmembers Karen Comstock and Chris Flores voted no. City Attorney Fred Galante had warned the council that murals carry free-speech protections that are hard to enforce evenly, citing a case where a county tried and failed to ban a mural it deemed disguised advertising. Flores raised the same concern about murals doubling as ads for smoke shops. (Champion, unrated local)
