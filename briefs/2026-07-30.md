# Daily Brief — Thursday, July 30, 2026

US and Saudi forces struck Iran-backed militias in Iraq, pulling a fourth country into the five-month Iran war. Japan's earthquake death toll climbed past 25. Chino Hills' sales tax measure is headed to the November ballot.

## International

**1. US and Saudi Arabia strike Iran-backed militias in Iraq.** CENTCOM and Saudi armed forces hit logistics and weapons sites in eastern Iraq Tuesday night, in response to more than 30 IRGC-directed drone attacks on Saudi oil facilities over the prior three days. Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces, a state-affiliated but Iran-aligned militia coalition, reported fighters killed and wounded in the strikes. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi called it "unacceptable aggression." The strikes widen the US-Iran war, now in its fifth month, into a fourth country. (NPR, Washington Times, lean right)

> *Psalm 46 does not promise the fighting stops on our timetable. It promises "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" while nations rage and kingdoms totter (v.1-6), then closes with "Be still, and know that I am God... I will be exalted among the nations" (v.10). That is a claim about who is not shaken, not a forecast of what happens next.*

**2. Russian barrage kills at least 8 civilians in Ukraine, including two children.** A ballistic missile and drone attack overnight killed at least eight and wounded dozens, among them two girls ages 5 and 12. Zelenskyy says Ukraine faces a "critical shortage" of Western air defense missiles, warning delayed deliveries are costing lives. Part of the shortage traces to the Iran war, which has drawn down US Patriot interceptor stockpiles. Trump has promised to let Ukraine build its own Patriots, but that production line does not exist yet. (NPR)

**3. Japan earthquake death toll climbs past 25.** A magnitude 7.1 quake hit Kumamoto Prefecture Tuesday. About 80 minutes later, an explosion, believed gas-related, tore through Aeon Mall in the town of Kashima after roughly 3,000 shoppers had already evacuated; part of the second floor collapsed. Additional deaths were reported at a paper mill in Yatsushiro. Tens of thousands of homes remain without power or running water. (NPR)

> *Luke 13:4-5. When eighteen died in the collapse of the tower of Siloam, Jesus refused to read it as proof those eighteen were worse sinners than anyone else. He turned it into a call to repentance for everyone listening, not a verdict on the dead. That is the right posture toward this story too: no reading of judgment onto strangers in a mall, only the same call to everyone still breathing.*

## National

**4. Fed holds rates steady, 9-3, over three dissents pushing for a hike.** The FOMC kept the federal funds rate at 3.5-3.75%. Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis' Neel Kashkari, and Dallas' Lorie Logan dissented in favor of raising rates, with inflation above the Fed's 2% target for more than five years running. It is the first real test for new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who has deliberately pulled back from the forward guidance his predecessors used. (NPR)

**5. Man convicted on federal terrorism charges over 2022 Rushdie stabbing.** Hadi Matar, already serving 25 years on a New York state attempted-murder conviction for the same attack, was convicted on federal terrorism charges tied to Khomeini's decades-old fatwa against Salman Rushdie. Prosecutors presented messages showing Matar discussing the fatwa online in 2021 and 2022. (NPR)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to the Nov. 3 ballot.** The city council placed a 1% sales tax increase before voters, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year. The city's 2026-27 general fund budget shows $59.2 million in revenue against $63.5 million in spending, a $4.3 million gap covered by reserves. Chino Hills has $25.4 million in unrestricted reserves and officials expect that cushion to run out within three to five years without new revenue. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. Chino council drops $1,230 planning appeal fee.** The council eliminated the fee residents were charged to appeal Planning Commission decisions, after resident Bob Nigg was billed it to contest the Eden project. Separately, more than 50 Chino residents were fined $1,000 each for illegal fireworks after police drone enforcement, and the Ramona-Francis annexation area remains an open issue before the council. (Champion, unrated local)
