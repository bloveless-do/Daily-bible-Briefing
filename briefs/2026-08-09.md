# Daily Brief — Sunday, August 9, 2026

Houthis hit a Saudi Aramco refinery days after Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan signed a mutual defense pact. The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general 50-49 after a bruising fight. Chino Hills' one-cent sales tax measure is set for the November ballot.

## International

**1. Houthis strike Saudi Aramco refinery as regional powers lock in a defense pact.** Yemen's Houthis claimed a drone strike on the Aramco refinery in Jizan, Saudi Arabia. A fire broke out and was extinguished; no injuries reported. The strike came two days after Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement on August 7, a mutual-defense pact treating an attack on any one signatory as an attack on all three. The pact builds on a 2025 Saudi-Pakistan agreement and was signed against the backdrop of the Iran war that began in February. (Reuters, NPR, Euronews)

**2. Iran nears a Hormuz shipping deal with Oman, still demanding US concessions.** Iran and Oman have been working out coordinates for a safe shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran says it has not held direct talks with Washington and is holding out for concessions before any reopening is finalized. (Reuters, Al Jazeera)

**3. Drone likely from Ukraine explodes in Bulgaria near a gas pipeline.** A drone crossed into Bulgarian airspace from Romania Saturday morning and detonated near the Trans-Balkan pipeline compressor station. Bulgaria's defense ministry said the wreckage matches a Ukrainian decoy drone type but found no evidence the strike was intentional. Ukraine denies targeting Bulgaria. No casualties or damage reported. Bulgaria's foreign minister summoned Ukraine's ambassador for a meeting Monday. (Washington Post, Euronews)

## National

**4. Senate confirms Todd Blanche as attorney general, 50-49.** The vote came after 4 a.m. Saturday, just ahead of the Senate's recess. Only Republicans Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski broke ranks to join all Democrats in opposition. Blanche, Trump's former personal defense lawyer, made a series of promises during the confirmation fight to lock down the GOP votes he needed. (Washington Post, NPR)

**5. Federal grants barred from funding drug test strips.** Trump administration officials told addiction treatment groups that federal grant money can no longer pay for the strips users rely on to check street drugs for fentanyl and other adulterants. Groups that distribute the strips say the change will cost lives. (Axios)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills' one-cent sales tax measure heads to the November 3 ballot.** The city council voted unanimously July 14 to place the Chino Hills Services Measure before voters. It would add a one-cent transactions and use tax, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for the general fund, exempting groceries, rent, prescriptions, utilities, and labor. Backers point to accountability provisions: an independent annual audit and public spending reports. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. Lithium-ion battery fire at Chino warehouse.** Boxes of lithium-ion batteries caught fire early Monday morning in a shipping warehouse on Ramona Avenue in Chino. No one was inside at the time and no injuries were reported. Cause remains under investigation. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the Blanche confirmation: reporting on the fight noted that opposition wasn't limited to Democrats, that the New York City Bar Association formally opposed the nomination, and that six senators raised conflict-of-interest concerns tied to Blanche shutting down crypto-industry investigations while at DOJ. Critics argue a defense attorney who personally represented Trump in a criminal case cannot credibly run the department that may investigate the president's allies, and that a 50-49 vote with two Republican defections is itself evidence the concerns were not fringe. (Washington Post, NPR)
