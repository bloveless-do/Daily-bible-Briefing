# Daily Brief — Friday, July 31, 2026

Trump says Hamas agreed to disarm in Gaza; Hamas says it hasn't. US strikes on Iran resumed Thursday and Congress rebuked the war a second time. Spain sent troops to Ceuta after 18 migrants died overnight. Locally, the Chino Hills sales tax measure is headed to the ballot and the FBI has been at a county supervisor's door in Chino Hills.

## International

**1. Trump claims a Gaza disarmament deal. Hamas disputes it.** Trump announced Thursday that his Board of Peace reached an agreement for Hamas to disarm and hand civilian control of Gaza to a new technocratic body, the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza. Neither Hamas nor Israel confirmed the announcement as Trump described it. A senior Hamas official told NPR the group will only disarm after Israel withdraws from Gaza, the opposite order from Trump's 20-point plan, which calls for Hamas to surrender weapons and destroy its tunnel network first. That sequencing fight is the same one that has stalled this process for months. (Axios, NPR)

**2. Iran war resumes, Congress rebukes it a second time.** The US launched a heavy wave of strikes Thursday on Iranian command centers and missile facilities, retaliating for an attempted Iranian ballistic missile attack on US forces Tuesday. Iran fired on Jordan's Muwaffaq Salti Air Base; Jordan shot down five missiles. Separately, US and Saudi strikes on Iran-backed militia bases in Iraq Wednesday killed at least 20 fighters and wounded 32; Iraq's president called it a violation of sovereignty and is pursuing legal action. The House passed a war powers resolution directing Trump to end the war for the second time, 215-208, with four Republicans joining Democrats. Brent crude is near $92 a barrel. (NPR, Axios)

**3. Spain deploys military to Ceuta after 18 migrants die.** Thousands of migrants breached the Morocco-Spain border fence into the Spanish territory of Ceuta Thursday. At least 18 died, most by drowning, some in a stampede at the Tarajal beach breakwater. Spain sent army units, a diving team, and a naval vessel Friday; Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez is traveling to Ceuta. (NPR)

## National

**4. Blanche's AG nomination in doubt.** Senate Judiciary Committee Republicans John Cornyn and Thom Tillis are withholding support over the Justice Department's settlement of Trump's IRS lawsuit, which the senators say they were denied written guarantees on. Trump said Thursday he "may" pull the nomination temporarily and keep Blanche on as acting attorney general, then renominate him later. (NPR)

**5. GDP growth slows, inflation stays elevated.** The economy grew at a 1.5% annual rate in the second quarter, down from 2.1% in the first quarter and below the 1.8% economists expected. Consumer spending held up at 2.1% growth. Prices in June were up 3.7% year over year, and inflation is outpacing wage gains. (NPR)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills sales tax headed to the Nov. 3 ballot.** The City Council voted 5-0 to place a one-cent sales tax measure on the November ballot, projected to raise $11.5 million a year. The measure gets its ballot letter from the Registrar of Voters on Aug. 10. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. FBI serves search warrants tied to county supervisor's Chino Hills home.** Federal agents served a search warrant at a Chino Hills property linked to San Bernardino County Supervisor Curt Hagman, along with locations tied to an Ontario councilman, an attorney, and a media executive. All warrants are sealed; the target of the investigation has not been disclosed. (KTLA, ABC7)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The skeptical case on the Gaza announcement: Trump's "historic agreement" language outran what either side actually confirmed. Israel and Hamas both stayed conspicuously silent Friday on the terms as Trump stated them, and Hamas official Ghazi Hamad said publicly the group won't move until Israel withdraws first, directly contradicting the phased sequence Trump described. Critics point out this is not the first time a Trump-announced "breakthrough" on Gaza has been billed as historic and then stalled on the exact same disarmament-before-withdrawal versus withdrawal-before-disarmament dispute. Read plainly, an announcement neither party has confirmed is not yet an agreement. (Washington Post)
