# Daily Brief — Friday, September 4, 2026

Iran and its Gulf neighbors traded fresh strikes after a monthlong lull in the war. The Nepal-China flood death toll passed 1,270. Gloria Steinem died at 92. Trump's team asked the Supreme Court to allow mail-in ballot restrictions before the midterms. Chino Hills' one-cent sales tax measure is headed to the November ballot.

## International

**1. Iran-US war flares again after monthlong pause.** Iran fired missiles and drones at US allies and bases across the Gulf early this week, retaliating for renewed American strikes on Iranian targets. Kuwait, Bahrain, Jordan and Iraq's Kurdistan region were all hit; Jordan said 13 ballistic missiles entered its airspace, 10 intercepted. The war, which began in late February, is in its seventh month and has repeatedly cycled through pauses and flare-ups. Oil markets have moved on the news. (NPR, The Hill)

**2. Nepal-China flood death toll passes 1,270.** A glacial collapse near Langtang Lirung sent rock, ice and meltwater into valleys in Nepal and Tibet in late August. Nepali authorities have recovered more than 1,250 bodies with over 4,200 still missing; Chinese authorities report 21 dead and 541 missing. Nearly 12,000 people have been rescued. Search efforts are into a second week, hampered by destroyed roads and landslides. (NPR)

## National

**3. Gloria Steinem dies at 92.** The feminist icon and Ms. magazine co-founder died Wednesday in New York, according to statements posted to her accounts. She spent six decades as the public face of the movement for workplace equality, abortion rights and sex education. (NPR)

**4. Trump administration asks Supreme Court to allow mail-in ballot restrictions before midterms.** The DOJ filed an emergency request Thursday to let the US Postal Service enforce new limits on mail ballots, bypassing lower courts still weighing the underlying case. The March executive order behind it directs DHS and the Social Security Administration to build a nationwide voter-eligibility list and requires states to get federal approval on mail-ballot envelope designs. About two dozen Democratic-led states have sued to block it. Supporters call it a fraud-prevention and list-maintenance measure; opponents call it a federal intrusion into state-run elections timed to suppress turnout two months before voters go to the polls. Both readings are live in the litigation itself, not just in the commentary. (NPR)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills' one-cent sales tax heads to Nov. 3 ballot.** The City Council voted 5-0 to place the "Chino Hills Services Measure" on the ballot. It would raise about $11.5 million a year, exempting groceries, rent, prescriptions and utility charges. The council cites a structural budget deficit and reserves projected to run out by fiscal year 2030-31 without new revenue. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Privacy concerns mount over Flock license-plate cameras in the Chino Valley.** Flock Safety's automated camera network, used by local law enforcement, has drawn scrutiny nationally over data access by immigration enforcement and misuse by individual officers. The company recently cut its standard data-retention window from 30 days to seven and added an opt-in audit-detection requirement for agencies. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The recurring case against the Iran war, pressed hardest in the New York Times' news analysis: after months of intermittent fighting, the US has secured few of its original strategic goals, Iran's regime remains intact and has adapted to the pressure, and each new round of strikes costs money and credibility without a defined end state. Critics inside and outside the administration's own coalition argue the war has become a war of choice without an exit plan. (NYT news analysis)
