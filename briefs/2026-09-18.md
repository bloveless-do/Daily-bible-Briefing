# Daily Brief — Friday, September 18, 2026

Trump threatens the EU with tariffs over Canada's proposed associate membership. Congress sends a sweeping Russia and Iran sanctions bill to Trump's desk. Hundreds of thousands rally in Iran in a government-staged show of defiance. Chino Hills' sales tax measure heads to the November ballot.

## International

**1. Trump threatens EU tariffs over Canada's proposed "associate membership."** Trump said he would impose "very serious tariffs" or halt trade with the EU if it follows through on European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen's proposal to make Canada the bloc's first associate member, calling the idea "laughable" and Canada "a terrible trade partner." The threat lands on top of an existing 50% US tariff on Canadian goods and planned bans on Canadian dairy, alcohol, and auto imports. (The Hill)

**2. Hundreds of thousands rally in Iran in government-organized show of defiance.** Iran's government staged a mass rally Friday, with state broadcasters estimating over 300,000 people in the streets and more than 600,000 registered to receive limited military training, the biggest such demonstration since the US and Israel struck Iran in February. The show of unity comes as Iran's economy buckles under a US naval blockade that has cut oil exports more than 80% since last year and sanctions that have pushed inflation above 80%. (AP; Bloomberg)

> *Psalm 2:1. Why do the nations rage? The psalm does not answer with a forecast. It answers with the One enthroned unshaken, and a blessing on those who take refuge in him. That is the whole of what this verse is for.*

**3. Russian barrage on Kyiv injures at least 19, including children.** Russian missiles and drones struck more than 10 locations in Kyiv and 33 sites across the wider region overnight, injuring at least 19 people including a 10-year-old girl and a 12-year-old boy. Ukrainian officials called it the highest number of children hurt in a single Kyiv attack since Russia's full-scale invasion began in 2022. Separately, Odesa saw seven more injured, two of them children. (AP)

## National

**4. Congress sends Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act to Trump's desk.** The House passed the bill 262-159, sending to Trump for signature a measure that lets the president impose tariffs on the largest buyers of Russian oil and gas (potentially including China and India), sanctions Russian banks and Putin-linked oligarchs, and extends sanctions authority against Iran's weapons and energy sectors. Fifty-eight Democrats joined most Republicans in favor; seven Republicans voted no. Trump is expected to sign it. (AP)

**5. White House withdraws ICE director nominee.** The administration pulled Lance Schroyer's nomination to lead ICE, a post that has gone without a Senate-confirmed director since 2017. Schroyer's nomination had stalled after Sen. Rand Paul, who chairs the relevant committee, said he wasn't satisfied with information the White House provided about the shooting of two US citizens by federal agents in Minnesota. (NPR)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to the November ballot.** The City Council placed Measure J on the Nov. 3 ballot, a 1-cent local sales tax that would raise the city's rate from 7.75% to 8.75% and is projected to generate about $11.5 million a year for city services. The measure includes required annual audits and public spending disclosure. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. Chino Valley Unified loses fight to lift injunction on parental-notification policy.** A San Bernardino County judge declined to dissolve the 2024 injunction blocking the district from enforcing a 2023 policy requiring staff to notify parents when a student identifies as transgender or asks to go by a different name or pronouns at school. The district says parents have a right to know what's happening with their kids; opponents say the policy puts students who aren't ready to come out at home at risk. The underlying case continues. (ABC7)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the EU tariff threat: critics argue the timing is self-defeating. The same week Congress sends Trump a bill built to squeeze Russia's oil revenue and support Ukraine, the administration threatens the EU, the bloc coordinating sanctions and arming Ukraine, over a largely symbolic membership gesture toward Canada. Fracturing Western unity over that gesture undercuts the leverage the sanctions bill is meant to build, and "terrible trade partner" rhetoric aimed at a longstanding ally reads less like strategy and more like improvisation. (Washington Post)
