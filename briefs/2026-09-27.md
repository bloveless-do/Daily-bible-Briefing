# Daily Brief — Sunday, September 27, 2026

Trump rejected Iran's seven-day ceasefire roadmap and expects strikes to resume after the midterms. Russia and Ukraine traded strikes that killed at least seven. Chino's council approved a 400-megawatt battery plant over a planning commission denial.

## International

**1. Trump rejects Iran's ceasefire roadmap, expects bombing to resume after midterms.** Iran's foreign minister presented a seven-day plan at the UN: reopen the Strait of Hormuz, lift the US naval blockade, waive sanctions on Iranian oil, and extend the ceasefire to Lebanon. Trump said Saturday, "I'm rejecting their deal," arguing Iran wants an immediate reopening "because they're losing so badly." He has told aides he expects the bombing campaign, running since February 28, to resume after the November midterms. Iran's president disputes that Tehran is waiting Washington out. (Axios, The Hill)

**2. Russia and Ukraine trade strikes, at least seven dead.** Russian strikes hit Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia, and the Odesa, Sumy, and Kharkiv regions Saturday, part of an intensifying pattern of cruise missiles, glide bombs, and drones more than four and a half years into the war. Zelenskyy said Russia is making its strikes "even more brutal" and called for a stronger international response. (The Hill/AP)

## National

**3. Supreme Court clears voter-verification database for use before midterms.** The court's conservative majority ruled 6-3 to lift a lower-court block on the Department of Homeland Security's SAVE database, letting states use it to check voter citizenship status ahead of November's elections. The system has a documented error rate: one Texas county found a quarter of its flagged "noncitizen" matches were people who had already proven US citizenship, and reporting out of a Missouri county found most of its flagged matches erroneous. The court noted federal law bars mass voter-roll purges close to an election, which limits the ruling's practical impact this cycle. (NPR, CNBC)

**4. White House drops CNN from the Air Force One press pool.** The White House swapped CNN for the conservative outlet Real America's Voice on Saturday's pool for Trump's trip to a Tennessee football game, the latest round in a running dispute. Other networks initially refused pool duty in protest; CNN was back in the pool days later for Trump's meeting with China's Xi Jinping. CNN, along with Politico and MS NOW, had also been barred from White House grounds last week before a judge granted a reprieve. (NPR, The Hill)

## Local and California

**5. Chino council overturns planning denial, approves 400-megawatt battery storage plant.** The council voted 4-1 to approve Aypa Power's "Dirac" battery energy storage project on 13.89 acres at the Southern California Edison substation on Benson Avenue, reversing the Planning Commission's 4-3 denial in July. Building trades unions, IBEW Local 477 and Ironworkers Local 433, backed the project citing roughly 168 union jobs; some residents and public-health groups opposed it. It would be the city's first battery storage facility. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Chino Hills: 1% sales tax measure heads to the November ballot.** The city council placed Measure J, a one-cent sales tax projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for local services, on the Nov. 3 ballot. The city clerk selected a resident's rebuttal argument for the Voter Information Guide, which will run alongside the council's argument in favor. (Champion, unrated local)
