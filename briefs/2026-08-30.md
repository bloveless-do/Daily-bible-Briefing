# Daily Brief — Sunday, August 30, 2026

A Russian strike near Kyiv killed 37 in the deadliest single attack of the war this year. Trump announced a sweeping U.S.-Venezuela oil deal that Democrats are calling corrupt. An FBI corruption probe continues to circle San Bernardino County Supervisor Curt Hagman, who represents Chino.

## International

**1. Russian strike on Kyiv-area depot kills 37, deadliest attack of the war this year.** A Russian strike on an ammunition depot in Myla, a village in Kyiv's Bucha district, triggered a detonation that killed 37 people late Friday, 34 of them residents of a care facility for the elderly and disabled nearby. President Zelensky vowed to investigate suspected negligence. The strike hit on the second of three straight days of near-constant Russian attacks on Kyiv and the surrounding region, which have also damaged at least 50 homes. It is the second such warehouse-detonation incident in two months. (NPR)

> *"God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble. Therefore we will not fear, though the earth give way" (Ps 46:1-2). That is not a comment on how or when the war ends. It is what the psalm actually offers: refuge now, not a forecast.*

**2. Iran war hits six-month mark with no end in sight.** Six months after the U.S. and Israel launched major combat operations against Iran, the conflict has settled into a stalemate. The U.S. still maintains its naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz while Iran negotiates with Oman on an alternate shipping route. Trump originally predicted a four-to-five-week war; the Trump administration has shifted toward economic pressure, including planned secondary sanctions, rather than further military escalation. (CNBC)

## National

**3. Trump announces "biggest oil deal in world history" with Venezuela.** Trump said the U.S. has secured majority control, an estimated 55% effective output, of Venezuelan oil fields holding 65 billion barrels of proven reserves, calling it a deal that will lower gas prices "at no cost to" taxpayers. Secretary of State Rubio and Defense Secretary Hegseth negotiated the terms. Senate Democrats Tim Kaine and Chris Van Hollen called it "corruption on an epic scale," arguing it enriches Trump allies and Big Oil rather than ordinary Americans or Venezuelans. (NPR, The Hill)

> *"He changes times and seasons; he removes kings and sets up kings" (Dan 2:21). That is a statement about who is sovereign over the rise and fall of rulers and regimes. It is not a claim about which side got the better end of this particular deal.*

**4. USPS appeals ruling blocking Trump's mail-in voting order, setting up a Supreme Court fight.** The Postal Service is appealing a federal judge's order that blocked its plan to implement Trump's executive order restricting mail-in ballot delivery ahead of the November 3 midterms. The Supreme Court already gave Trump an interim win allowing parts of the order to proceed, but neither that ruling nor the appeals court decision below has settled the policy's underlying legality. Challengers can still seek new injunctions once a final rule is in place. (NPR)

## Local and California

**5. FBI corruption probe continues around Supervisor Curt Hagman.** Federal agents served search warrants at the Chino home and office of San Bernardino County Supervisor Curt Hagman in late July, part of a public corruption investigation reportedly examining possible foreign influence. Four others were raided the same day, including Ontario Councilman Alan Wapner, Hagman's fellow officer on the Ontario International Airport Authority board, which was also served a grand jury subpoena. Hagman has stepped down from some boards and committees but not his supervisor seat. The fallout has already reshaped the Ontario City Council race. No charges have been filed. (Champion, unrated local; ABC7)

> *"For nothing is hidden that will not be disclosed, nor is anything secret that will not become known" (Lk 8:17). Said about hidden things generally, not this case specifically. No charges yet means no verdict yet either way.*

**6. Chino Hills one-cent sales tax measure headed to Nov. 3 ballot.** The City Council voted 5-0 to place a permanent one-cent Transactions and Use Tax on the ballot, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for public safety, wildfire response, and general city services. Mayor Brian Johsz, who says he usually votes no on tax increases, backed this one, citing the city's reliance on reserves to cover operating costs. An opposition argument has been filed for the Voter Information Guide. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the Venezuela oil deal: critics note the U.S. government may need to sink significant capital for the arrangement to produce oil anywhere near the claimed 65 billion barrels, and one analysis pegs Venezuela's actual take at roughly $3.20 a barrel, far below market value. The deal was negotiated without Venezuela's opposition at the table, and by locking in U.S. interests with the current government for up to a century, it may reduce Washington's incentive to push for genuine elections there. In short: a headline number that may not survive contact with the execution, structured in a way that favors continuity of the current Caracas leadership over reform. (Washington Post, Bloomberg)
