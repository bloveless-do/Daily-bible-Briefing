## International

**1. Trump defends Iran war at UN General Assembly, offers no exit timeline.** Trump addressed world leaders Tuesday and framed the nearly eight-month war with Iran as a success, even as it has hardened into a stalemate that has not achieved his stated goals, depleted Pentagon missile stockpiles, and driven his approval below 40 percent amid the economic fallout. His UN ambassador said the speech would also cover the Gaza peace plan and Western Hemisphere policy. Iran's Revolutionary Guard warned it would "change the geography of the war" if the US escalates further. (NPR, Reuters)

**3. Houthi gains in Yemen tighten the Red Sea chokepoint.** Iran-backed Houthi forces have pushed to seize strategic heights cutting off Red Sea coastline from Saudi-coalition-held areas, adding new pressure on shipping and global energy prices as the broader Iran conflict grinds on. Saudi Arabia struck the port city of Mokha in response. (Reuters)

## National

**4. CNN, MS NOW, and Politico sue Trump administration over White House ban.** The three outlets and three of their reporters sued after being blocked from White House grounds last week, alleging First Amendment and due-process violations. A federal judge set a videoconference hearing for Wednesday. Major TV networks have since refused to provide pool coverage of the president in solidarity. (NPR, CNBC)

**5. FAA launches AI-driven airspace management system.** The FAA rolled out SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories), part of an $875 million contract with Air Space Intelligence, starting at three airports serving the DC region. The system uses machine learning to predict traffic imbalances and recommend routing before planes leave the gate. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said it is meant to reduce delays and controller workload, not replace controllers. (NPR)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills' Measure J sales tax heads to the November ballot.** The city council placed a 1 percent sales tax increase on the Nov. 3 ballot, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year in locally controlled revenue. It would exempt prescriptions, labor, rent, education, utilities, and groceries. The move follows a 2026-27 General Fund budget with a projected $4.3 million deficit covered by reserves. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. Chino City Council rejects developers' bid to skip undergrounding power lines.** The council denied appeals from Warmington Homes, on behalf of Regent Land Company and BCT Development Acquisition Company, seeking to waive a requirement to bury overhead utility lines at two Riverside Drive housing projects totaling roughly 100 units. Developers cited added connection costs; the council held the line. (Champion, unrated local)
