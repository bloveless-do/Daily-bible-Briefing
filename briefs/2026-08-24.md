# Daily Brief — Monday, August 24, 2026

Treasury unveils "economic D-Day" sanctions on Iran as the war grinds into its sixth month. Syria and Israel met in Jordan to de-escalate. US-Canada trade talks collapsed, 50% tariffs now in effect. Rand Paul is blocking the ICE director hearing over the Minneapolis killings. Chino Hills' sales tax measure heads to the November ballot.

## International

**1. Treasury unveils "economic D-Day" sanctions on Iran, war in its sixth month.** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent held a Monday press conference to detail what he called the toughest sanctions in history on Iran, aimed at cutting off "every economic lifeline" propping up Tehran, including nations still buying Iranian oil or handling its ships. The push follows the collapse of an April ceasefire after Iran struck three commercial vessels in July; the US naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has been back in place since. Iran's rial hit a record low on the news. Iran's national security chief said Tehran will "retaliate in a seismic manner" and treat any nation backing the sanctions as committing an act of war. (NPR, Reuters)

**2. Syria and Israel meet in Jordan to de-escalate.** Senior Syrian and Israeli delegations, led by Syria's foreign minister Asaad al-Shaibani and Mossad chief Roman Gofman, held their first direct contact in months in Amman on Sunday, aimed at halting Israeli strikes inside Syria and reviving talks toward a security agreement. The meeting followed an Aug. 18 Israeli strike on a Syrian airbase that Israel said was pre-empting a Turkish deployment there. Al-Shaibani told Reuters that Damascus does not trust Israel but that the door remains open to diplomacy. (Reuters)

## National

**3. US-Canada trade talks collapse, 50% tariffs take effect.** Talks between Washington and Ottawa broke down before a weekend deadline. The US imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods, including hockey sticks, building materials, liquor, and clothing. Prime Minister Mark Carney called the US terms "uneconomic, unfair" and pledged dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs starting Sept. 8. (Bloomberg)

**4. Rand Paul blocks ICE director's confirmation hearing over Minneapolis killings.** The Senate Homeland Security chair is withholding a hearing for ICE nominee Lance Schroyer until the administration discloses more about its own investigation into the killings of two American citizens by federal agents in Minneapolis in January: Renee Good, a mother watching an immigration operation from her car, and Alex Pretti, a nurse and lawful gun owner who was filming the agents. Paul, a Republican, says DHS and ICE need to restore trust rather than litigate the narrative. Supporters of the agents say the shootings are still under review and shouldn't be prejudged; families and some lawmakers in both parties say two dead citizens is reason enough to slow-walk the confirmation until answers are public. (Politico, The Hill)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to the Nov. 3 ballot.** The city has placed a one-cent sales tax on the November ballot to shore up the budget. City Manager Rod Hill has been briefing community groups this month on the case for it. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the Iran sanctions push: the one lever with real teeth is squeezing the Chinese refiners and banks that move Iranian oil, but pulling it risks a Beijing retaliation on critical minerals exports right as Trump prepares to host Xi Jinping next month. Oil prices already climbed more than 5% this week on the announcement alone, a cost that lands on American drivers before it lands on Tehran. Critics also point to the pattern: every claim of a decisive, unprecedented blow is followed by a bigger one a few weeks later, which looks less like a plan closing in on an endpoint and more like a strategy that hasn't found one yet. (Bloomberg)
