# Daily Brief - Saturday, August 29, 2026

Trump announces a US stake in 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil, and critics call the "no cost to taxpayers" line misleading. The Nepal-China border flood death toll hits 616 with 2,500 still missing. In Chino Hills, the surviving suspect in a kidnapping-killing pleads not guilty, and Chino's battery storage fight drags on past a planning commission denial.

## International

**1. US claims majority stake in Venezuelan oil reserves.** Trump announced Friday that the US has finalized a deal with Venezuela giving Washington effective majority control of oil fields holding 65 billion barrels of proven reserves. The deal, negotiated by Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth with Venezuela's interim president Delcy Rodriguez, sets up a new private joint venture in which the US holds a 55% stake, including rights to buy oil at cost. Trump says it comes "at no cost to the American taxpayer." Independent analysts dispute that, saying oil majors have little incentive to invest in Venezuela's degraded industry without a government-backed subsidy that could run past $100 billion. (Axios, NPR)

**2. Iran's regime still standing six months after the war.** Saturday marks roughly six months since the ceasefire ended the US-Israel bombing campaign against Iran that began in February. Regime change was a stated goal early in the war; Iran's government has not collapsed. Talks toward a formal end to the conflict stalled after Iran struck three commercial vessels in July, and negotiations remain on hold. (NPR)

**3. Nepal-China border flood death toll rises to 616.** Nepali police said Saturday morning the toll from last Wednesday's flash flood at the Nepal-China border has risen to 616, with roughly 2,500 people still unaccounted for on both sides of the border, including dozens of Americans. The flood was triggered by a glacier and rock collapse into a river in Nepal's Rasuwa region. More than 2,300 people are being treated for injuries. (AP)

## National

**4. Judge rules Pentagon's Anthropic blacklist was illegal retaliation.** A federal judge in San Francisco ruled this week that the Pentagon's designation of AI company Anthropic as a "supply chain risk" was retaliation for the company's public objections to Pentagon plans for its models, not a genuine security judgment. US District Judge Rita Lin found the designation violated Anthropic's First Amendment rights and made permanent a block first imposed in March. A separate, narrower Anthropic challenge over a related federal procurement rule is still pending in the DC Circuit. (NPR, Axios)

**5. Diesel near $5.60 a gallon squeezes school district budgets.** Diesel prices, pushed up by Iran-war disruption to fuel markets, are forcing school districts to cut bus routes as the new year starts. AAA put the national average at $5.62 a gallon this week, up from $3.70 a year ago. A May survey found more than half of districts already over fuel budget before the school year began. Yakima, Washington's district estimates a 38% hike in diesel costs this year, about $130,000, and has consolidated routes and staggered bell times to cope. (NPR)

**6. Judge rejects Trump's third bid to move hush money case to federal court.** A federal judge in Manhattan rejected Trump's latest attempt to move his 2024 hush money conviction to federal court, where his lawyers argued broader immunity claims would apply. Judge Alvin Hellerstein said Trump's team waited too long after the verdict to seek the move. The conviction, on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records, stands; Trump was sentenced to an unconditional discharge with no punishment. (Washington Post)

## Local and California

**7. Chino Hills kidnapping-murder suspect pleads not guilty.** The surviving suspect in a Chino Hills kidnapping and killing pleaded not guilty to murder this month in a Rancho Cucamonga courtroom. Jianquan Bo, 66, is held without bail. Prosecutors say Bo's brother, Zhengfeng Bo, shot 60-year-old Shukur Aikebaer in the trunk of a car in front of sheriff's deputies after the pair allegedly tampered with Aikebaer's electrical panel to lure him outside; deputies shot and killed Zhengfeng at the scene. San Bernardino County DA Jason Anderson says Jianquan knew the plan and participated enough to be charged with murder himself. (ABC7, CBS Los Angeles)

**8. Chino's battery storage fight isn't over.** The Chino Planning Commission voted 4-3 in July to deny Aypa Power's proposed 400-megawatt Dirac Energy Storage project, across the street from Ayala Park next to a Southern California Edison substation. Opponents cited fire, chemical leak, and noise risks, and noted the city has no setback ordinance since this would be its first battery storage facility. Aypa has appealed the denial; no city council hearing date has been set. (Champion, unrated local; CBS Los Angeles)

## Steelman - the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the Venezuela oil deal: critics say Trump's "no cost to the taxpayer" line hides the real bill. Analysts note oil majors have little market incentive to invest in Venezuela's degraded infrastructure without a government backstop, and Trump has floated federal reimbursement for companies' rebuilding costs, an outlay Rep. Mike Levin's Protecting Taxpayers from Risky Investments in Venezuela Act is trying to head off before it happens. Critics also point out the deal was struck by the same administration that removed Maduro by force nine months ago and now negotiates unilaterally with the interim government it installed, raising sovereignty and legality questions well beyond an ordinary business transaction. And reporting on the deal notes the added Venezuelan barrels are unlikely to move US gas prices much at all, undercutting the deal's central selling point. (Washington Post, Axios, NPR)
