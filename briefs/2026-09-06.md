# Daily Brief — Sunday, September 6, 2026

The US-Iran war grinds into month seven with fresh Gulf strikes after a US strike hit a wedding. Both parties are now running ads against data centers ahead of the midterms. Chino Hills' sales tax measure is locked in for the November ballot.

## International

**1. US-Iran war hits day 190, fighting spreads to Gulf bases.** The war, which began February 28, has widened. Iran's military claimed missile and drone strikes on Ahmed al-Jaber Air Base in Kuwait and Al Minhad Air Base in the UAE, retaliation the Pentagon tied to a US strike that hit a home hosting a wedding in Kuhestak, near the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian state media reported four killed, including two children, and dozens wounded. Trump said the strike followed a failed Iranian attempt to mine the strait; US Central Command said it hit Iranian air defense, radar, and maritime targets. Saudi Arabia has taken the heaviest regional toll outside Iran, hit by at least 38 missiles and hundreds of drones since the war began. (NPR, Washington Post news)

**2. Gaza ceasefire keeps fraying.** Despite the October 2025 truce, Israeli strikes and incursions continue. This week Israeli forces shelled parts of Gaza City and fired toward tents sheltering displaced Palestinians. Gaza health officials say roughly 1,270 people have been killed by Israeli strikes since the ceasefire took effect, a toll Israel says includes responses to individual Hamas violations. Ten people were killed Wednesday in strikes that came two days after US negotiators reportedly asked Israel to scale back operations. (NPR)

## National

**3. Both parties are running ads against data centers.** An NPR review of AdImpact data found more than $45 million spent since January on campaign ads mentioning data centers in this year's governor, House, and Senate races, split almost evenly: over $22 million backing Republicans, more than $21 million backing Democrats. Both sides make the same pitch, that the facilities get outsized tax breaks and drive up electricity bills. Ads have run for Democrat David Crowley in Wisconsin, Republican Byron Donalds in Florida, Democrat Rob Sand in Iowa, and Republican Ty Masterson in Kansas. (NPR)

**4. DeSantis bans license-plate readers on Florida state roads.** The Florida Department of Transportation revoked permits for local police to run automated license-plate cameras on state roadways and ordered existing units removed within 30 days, after DeSantis called the surveillance "out of control." Miami-Dade suspended its entire program the next day. The state had approved $4.6 million in grants for roughly 440 of the readers as recently as last year. (CBS News)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills sales tax measure locked in for November.** The city council voted unanimously in July to place Measure J on the November 3 ballot, a 1% local sales tax projected to raise $11.5 million a year against a projected $4.3 million structural budget shortfall. Groceries, rent, utilities, prescriptions, and labor are exempt. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Chino battery storage project still drawing protest.** Aypa Power's proposal for the city's first battery energy storage facility, on 13.89 acres across from Ayala Park, drew a small protest at Central and Chino avenues with signs reading "No BESS" and "Don't blow up our community." The city has not yet ruled on the project. (Champion, unrated local)
