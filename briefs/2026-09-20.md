# Daily Brief — Sunday, September 20, 2026

Ukraine hit Moscow's oil refinery in the war's largest drone barrage yet, timed to Russia's parliamentary election. ## International

**1. Ukraine's largest drone attack of the war hits Moscow.** Russia's defense ministry said air defenses downed 1,110 drones overnight, the biggest barrage of the war and well past August's previous high of 822. One drone struck the Moscow oil refinery in Kapotnya, which supplies about 40% of the capital's fuel and 70% of the region's petrol and aviation gas. The refinery caught fire; no injuries were reported. The strike landed on the final day of Russia's parliamentary election. Russia hit back overnight with dozens of drones and two antiship missiles into Ukraine. (Bloomberg, NPR)

**2. Houthi forces complete takeover of Yemen's Red Sea coast.** The Iran-backed Houthis now hold the full coastline from Hodeidah south through the port of Mokha to the Bab el-Mandeb strait, through which about 12% of world trade passes. Strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure forced the shutdown of a major pipeline, and the escalation has pushed oil above $100 a barrel. (NPR)

> *Psalm 46:9 says God "makes wars cease to the end of the earth." That is not a forecast for this strait or this week. It is a claim about who holds the outcome, read alongside the fact that oil markets are pricing real risk right now.*

**3. UN lets Abbas address General Assembly by video again after US denies visa.** The General Assembly voted 152-3 (the US, Israel, and Paraguay against, four abstaining) to let Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas speak by prerecorded video for a second consecutive year, after Washington again refused him a visa. Abbas is set to deliver the address when the high-level debate opens Sept. 22. (AP via U.S. News)

## National

**4. Trump bars CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House.** Trump announced Friday he was banning the three outlets "effective immediately," saying they report "fake news" about him; he cited no specific story. Reporters and a photographer from all three were denied entry at a West Wing security gate Saturday morning and had press badges confiscated. CNN, Politico, and MS NOW say they will pursue legal action; Politico also disputes Trump's claim that it receives government subsidies. First Amendment lawyers, including the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press and the Knight First Amendment Institute, call the move viewpoint discrimination that is likely to be struck down if challenged in court. (Axios, NPR)

**5. House holds Leon Black in contempt of Congress over the Epstein probe.** The House voted by unanimous consent to hold billionaire investor Leon Black in contempt for defying two subpoenas in the Oversight Committee's Epstein investigation, after the committee voted 41-0 to recommend it. Black is suing the committee and Chair James Comer to block the subpoenas; that case is pending in federal court. The contempt finding sets up a possible criminal referral to the Justice Department, carrying up to a year in prison and a $100,000 fine if Black is convicted. Black's attorneys call the probe a "fishing expedition" and say he had no knowledge of Epstein's conduct. (NPR)

> *Proverbs 28:13: the one who conceals his sins does not prosper, but the one who confesses and forsakes them finds mercy. That is the standard the subpoena is testing, not a verdict on Black.*

**6. Delaware judge orders Fox Corp to hand over Murdoch documents.** A Delaware judge ruled Friday that Fox Corp must turn over roughly 700 documents, including board records and executive emails, to shareholders suing over how Rupert Murdoch and the board handled scandals including the Dominion defamation case and phone-hacking allegations at other Murdoch properties. The suit, filed three years ago, argues the board let a scandal-prone business model repeatedly expose the company to costly litigation. (NPR)

## Local and California

**7. Chino Hills' one-cent sales tax measure heads to the Nov. 3 ballot.** Measure J would raise the local sales tax rate by one cent, projected to bring in $11.5 million a year for the city. The city clerk selected the official opposition argument for the voter guide, submitted by resident Bill Shannon: it argues the city already has grant funding and a paid lobbyist, that the tax falls on every resident rather than just those who skip a landscape fee, and that tax increases invite more of the same. The council's argument in favor cites a landscape and parks maintenance deficit. (Champion, unrated local)

**8. Chino Council floats new memorial park.** At the Sept. 1 meeting, Councilmember Curtis Burton proposed developing a park named for the late Russell M. Miller Sr. No site or budget has been set. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the press ban: this is not a stylistic complaint about coverage, it is the government using physical access to punish outlets over content, the exact move the First Amendment exists to block. Legal scholars are not split on the basic point. Jameel Jaffer of Columbia's Knight First Amendment Institute called it "pretty clearly unconstitutional." Press-freedom groups argue that once the White House opens its doors to a press pool, it cannot then pick and choose based on viewpoint, and that the subsidy claim used to single out Politico does not hold up. If the ban stands unchallenged, it is a precedent any future administration, of either party, can reach for against unfriendly coverage. (NPR)
