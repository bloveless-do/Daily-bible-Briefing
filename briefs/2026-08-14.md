# Daily Brief — Friday, August 14, 2026

A new carrier is steaming toward the Middle East to relieve a strained USS Abraham Lincoln. A federal judge blocked Trump's mail-in voting order nationwide ahead of the midterms. An FBI raid's fallout pushed a San Bernardino County supervisor off his boards and commissions.

## International

**1. USS George Washington heads to the Middle East to relieve the Lincoln.** The Navy is sending the George Washington, its only forward-deployed carrier, to the Arabian Sea to replace the USS Abraham Lincoln, which has been deployed more than 250 days without a port call. Reports describe severe strain on the crew, including attempts by some sailors to jump overboard. The swap keeps two carriers in the region alongside the USS George H.W. Bush as part of the pressure campaign tied to Iran and the Strait of Hormuz. Treasury Secretary Bessent said Thursday the administration would soon add economic pressure on Iran. (NPR, WSJ)

**2. Ukrainian strike hits Russian naval base, halts grain terminals.** A Ukrainian attack on Novorossiysk, a Black Sea port, struck warships at a naval base and forced two major grain terminals to stop operating. Chicago wheat futures rose about 3 percent on the news. (Reuters)

## National

**3. Luigi Mangione pleads guilty in federal case.** Mangione pleaded guilty Friday to two federal stalking charges tied to the 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, telling the judge, "I shot Mr. Thompson in Manhattan." Each charge carries a possible life sentence. His state murder trial in New York is still set to start next month, and the judge said a state conviction could be added to his federal sentence. (AP, NPR)

> *Romans 13:4, the governing authority "does not bear the sword in vain." A confessed killer facing a federal courtroom and a state trial next month is the ordinary working of that verse, not a departure from it.*

**4. Federal judge expands nationwide block on Trump's mail-in voting order.** U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani, an Obama appointee, barred the Postal Service from implementing provisions of Trump's executive order that would have let USPS refuse to deliver mail ballots in states that did not build citizenship-verification lists with the agency. Talwani ruled the Constitution's Elections Clause gives that power to states and Congress, not the executive, and said blocking it was necessary with the midterms less than 90 days out. The administration says the order targets noncitizen voting; state election officials say existing verification processes already make that rare. The order's first version was blocked in June and is now pending before the Supreme Court. (Axios, Bloomberg, Washington Examiner, lean right)

## Local and San Bernardino County

**5. FBI raid fallout reaches County Supervisor Curt Hagman.** Hagman's Chino home and Chino Hills office were among the sites raided by the FBI on July 28, along with the Chino office of developer Andy Sehremelis of Parkcrest Construction, which holds a 30-year county lease on 40 acres at Chino Airport. No charges have been announced against Hagman. He has stepped away from all boards, commissions, and committees he sits on and said he will return a $1,000 campaign contribution he received from Sehremelis. (Champion, unrated local)

> *Proverbs 11:3, "The integrity of the upright guides them, but the crookedness of the treacherous destroys them." No verdict has been reached on anyone named in the raid. The proverb describes the shape of the thing, not a ruling on Hagman.*

**6. Chino Hills one-cent sales tax measure headed to the Nov. 3 ballot.** The city council voted 5-0 to place the Chino Hills Services Measure on the ballot. It would add a 1 percent local sales tax, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for public safety, wildfire response, and road maintenance. Letters to the editor in the Champion show organized local opposition already forming. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case for Judge Talwani's ruling: the Elections Clause reserves election rules to states and Congress, not the executive, and noncitizen voting is already rare under existing state verification processes. Critics argue the order's proof-of-citizenship push manufactures a problem to justify federal control over ballot delivery less than 90 days before a contested midterm, when any last-minute rule change falls hardest on voters who lack quick access to new paperwork. (Washington Post, NPR)
