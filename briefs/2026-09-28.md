# Daily Brief — Monday, September 28, 2026

Trump rejected Iran's seven-day ceasefire offer and is weighing a post-midterm bombing decision. Five men arrested near a UK air base used in the Iran strikes. Chino Hills' 1% sales tax measure heads to the November ballot.

## International

**1. Trump rejects Iran's ceasefire proposal, considers renewed strikes after midterms.** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks within seven days if the US ends what Tehran calls its naval blockade and economic pressure. Trump rejected it Saturday: "They made a proposal but I rejected it." He has told aides he sees a return to major bombing as likely after the November elections, and told Axios last week he faces a "big decision" on whether to "annihilate" the Iranian regime. The war is in its seventh month. (NPR, WSJ, Axios)

> *"Why do the nations rage?" (Ps 2:1). The psalm does not tell you what Trump or Iran does next. It ends with the One enthroned unshaken and a warning to rulers to serve him with fear. That is the whole claim, no more.*

**2. Five arrested near UK air base used in Iran strikes.** British police evacuated 85 homes and arrested five men near RAF Fairford, about 90 minutes outside London, after reports of suspicious vehicles heading toward the base around 12:45 a.m. Sunday. A bomb disposal unit searched the vehicles under a 400-meter cordon. The men were arrested on explosives offenses and later on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act. Investigators are examining a possible Iran connection; officials have not confirmed one. (NPR, BBC)

## National

**3. Trump administration weighs diesel export ban as fuel prices climb before midterms.** With cost of living topping voter concerns in recent polling, the administration is considering restricting diesel exports, possibly for 90 days, to bring down domestic prices. The Chamber of Commerce, Business Roundtable, and American Petroleum Institute warned in a joint letter that a ban would raise, not lower, retail prices and invite retaliation from trade partners. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the administration is looking at restrictions short of an outright ban. (Politico, WSJ)

> *"Many seek the favor of a ruler, but justice for man comes from the LORD" (Prov 29:26). That cuts both ways: the temptation to manage prices for an election and the temptation to assume the market alone settles what is just.*

**4. Cook Political Report: Democrats favored to retake the House.** Cook founder Charlie Cook called a Democratic House majority after November a "done deal" conclusion, with Senate control also "very realistic" for Democrats. Seven months of war and rising fuel costs are cited as headwinds for Republican incumbents. (The Hill, Axios)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to November ballot.** The city council placed Measure J on the Nov. 3 ballot, a 1% local sales tax projected to raise about $11.5 million a year. The city cites inflation, a new high/very-high fire hazard severity designation, aging infrastructure, and rising public safety costs as the reasons behind the shortfall. The measure includes required annual audits and public spending reports. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Chino Valley Unified loses fight to keep parental notification policy as written.** A San Bernardino County Superior Court judge kept in place an injunction against the district's original policy, which required staff to notify parents if a child asked to be identified by a different gender than on official records. Board President Sonja Shaw said staff will keep notifying parents under an amended policy that drops the gender-specific language and instead requires notification for changes to any student record. Parental-rights advocates read this as continuity of a parent's right to know; opponents of the original policy read it as an attempt to route around the injunction. Both readings are represented in ongoing coverage. (ABC7 Inland Empire)
