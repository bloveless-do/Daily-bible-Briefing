# Daily Brief — Thursday, August 13, 2026

Iran disputes Trump's claim of controlling the Strait of Hormuz as shipping traffic hits a three-month low. Colombia's earthquake death toll passed 265. Karoline Leavitt is stepping down as White House press secretary.

## International

**1. Iran disputes Trump's Hormuz claims as shipping traffic bottoms out.** Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority said Wednesday the Strait of Hormuz "remains blocked and will not be reopened until Iran's conditions are accepted," directly contradicting Trump's repeated claims that the US has "total control" of the waterway. Ship transits fell to a five-day average of about 13 vessels, near a three-month low and roughly 90% below the 130-ship daily average before the war started in February. The interim deal the two sides signed June 17 to reopen the strait has collapsed, with both governments trading blame over shipping-route terms nearly six months into the war. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the US "has long miscalculated due to intelligence failures." (Reuters, The Hill)

**2. Colombia earthquake death toll passes 265.** The magnitude 7.4 quake that struck western Colombia Monday has now killed at least 265 people, President Abelardo de la Espriella said Wednesday. Pereira, Cali, Quibdó, Manizales and Armenia remain under the country's highest disaster alert. Rescue crews are working a fourth day past the point where survival odds drop sharply. (AP, NPR)

> *Lamentations 3:22-23. The steadfast love of the LORD never ceases, his mercies never come to an end, they are new every morning. Not an explanation for the quake, a word for crews still digging on day four.*

**3. Israel strikes Hamas commander in Gaza, breaking a week-long lull.** Israel's military said a drone strike killed a Hamas commander in Beit Lahiya on Wednesday, the first strike in the territory in more than a week. It comes after Netanyahu rejected the disarmament terms Hamas accepted under the US-backed peace plan, saying Hamas must fully disarm before Israel signs on. (AP)

## National

**4. Karoline Leavitt stepping down as White House press secretary.** Trump announced Wednesday that Leavitt, 28 and the youngest press secretary in US history, will leave the role by the end of August. Leavitt said she could not give her two young children, including a daughter born in May, the attention they deserve while holding the job. Trump said she will remain a "top outside advisor" heading into the midterms. (Axios, The Hill)

**5. White House construction spending swells past $900 million.** The Washington Post reported Wednesday that Trump administration construction plans for the White House grounds, including the East Wing demolition, a new ballroom, an underground security complex and a West Wing redesign, now total at least $900 million, up from an earlier $600 million estimate. The money is being routed through agency transfers and private donations into a maintenance account rather than through direct congressional appropriation. (The Hill; also Washington Post)

**6. Trump administration presses Supreme Court again on mail-ballot order.** The Justice Department asked the Supreme Court on Wednesday to let it fully implement Trump's executive order restricting mail-in ballots ahead of November's midterms, after a federal judge in Boston blocked the Postal Service from enforcing it nationwide, the second such injunction. Solicitor General D. John Sauer asked the justices to "act promptly." Twenty-three states and DC are suing over the order. (Axios)

Faithful Christians land on different sides of the order itself. Supporters argue states have let mail-ballot rules go too loose and a federal eligibility check restores confidence in the count. Opponents argue the Constitution gives states and Congress, not the president, authority over federal election rules, and that a citizenship list run through DHS and SSA invites errors that wrongly strip eligible voters. Two federal appeals courts have sided with the states so far.
