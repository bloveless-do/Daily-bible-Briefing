# Daily Brief — Friday, August 21, 2026

Trump declares "economic warfare" on Iran as oil jumps. A charter plane crash kills 8 in western Alaska. Chino police confront a troubled 1,645-unit apartment complex; the Hagman FBI-raid fallout continues.

## International

**1. Trump declares "economic warfare" on Iran, oil prices jump.** President Trump announced what he called the "most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country" against Iran, threatening penalties on any nation that helps Iran evade sanctions. The stated goal is to force Tehran to end its nuclear program and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Vice President Vance said the war has entered a "new phase" of economic pressure. Oil prices rose more than 2%, hitting about $94 a barrel, the highest since late July. (The Hill, Axios)

**2. Israel strikes Syrian air base to block Turkish deployment, straining a three-way US partnership.** Israel carried out roughly eight airstrikes Tuesday on the Abu al-Duhur air base in northwest Syria, hitting the runway and storage facilities. No casualties were reported. Netanyahu's office said the strike was meant to stop Turkish troops from deploying there, calling it a breach of an agreed status quo. Syria and Turkey condemned the strikes as a sovereignty violation. Syria's foreign minister told Axios that Syria was not coordinating with Turkey to build up forces at the base. US envoy Tom Barrack called the strike an "unnecessary escalation." (NPR, Axios)

**3. China sentences Evergrande founder to life in prison.** A Shenzhen court sentenced Hui Ka Yan, founder of collapsed property giant Evergrande, to life in prison for fraud and bribery. He pleaded guilty in April to eight charges, including fundraising fraud and illegal securities issuance. More than 50 people connected to Evergrande, including Hui's two sons, were also sentenced. Evergrande entities were fined about $2.35 billion combined. (NPR)

## National

**4. Charter plane crash kills 8 at remote Alaska radar site.** A Cessna 441 operated by Security Aviation crashed Thursday near Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site Airport in western Alaska, about 450 miles west of Anchorage. Two pilots and six passengers were aboard. US Alaskan Command confirmed no survivors. (NPR)

**5. Tens of thousands in Indiana enter a second week without power.** An Aug. 11 storm with 99 mph winds toppled trees and power lines across northwest Indiana. As of Thursday, roughly 75,000 Northern Indiana Public Service Co. customers remained without power, most in Lake County. Gary's school district has been closed since Aug. 13, and full restoration there isn't expected until Aug. 25. About 300 utility poles need repair or replacement. (NPR)

**6. FBI seizes former Rep. Eric Swalwell's devices in sexual-misconduct probe.** Federal agents executed search warrants the weekend of Aug. 15-16, one at San Francisco International Airport when Swalwell landed, another at his Washington residence. Court filings show agents seized an iPhone and a MacBook Pro. The probe concerns alleged civil-rights violations tied to sexual misconduct allegations that led Swalwell to resign from Congress and drop his gubernatorial bid in April. (Forbes, NPR)

## Local and California

**7. Chino police confront a troubled 1,645-unit apartment complex.** Chino police are engaging with residents of Homecoming Apartments in The Preserve after rising security concerns. Police Chief Kevin Mensen said the department responded to 23 child-neglect calls from January through June 2026 and averaged 31 domestic-disturbance calls a month over that span. In 2025, police responded to 1,098 calls for service at the complex, about three a day. Developer Lewis Management Co. has installed 40 security cameras, with feed access given to the police department's Real Time Crime Center. (Champion, unrated local)

**8. Hagman FBI-raid fallout continues to ripple through local politics.** Fourth District San Bernardino County Supervisor Curt Hagman, whose Chino home and Chino Hills office were raided by the FBI on July 28, has told the board he will skip meetings and is stepping away from committees, commissions, and boards for now. The FBI also raided the Chino office of developer Andrea "Andy" Sehremelis of Parkcrest Construction. Ontario Councilman Alan Wapner, also raided, dropped his mayoral bid for a council run instead, and Hagman's son Jonathan withdrew from a District 3 race. (Champion, unrated local)
