US officials say China's rare earth deliveries "have not been up to par," and Beijing is behind on its pledge to buy $17 billion in farm goods beyond soybeans, where it is on track. The two leaders are expected to meet again at APEC in Shenzhen in November and the G20 in Miami in December. (Bloomberg, Reuters)

## National

**4. White House keeps CNN, MS NOW, and Politico locked out despite a judge's order to let them back in.** A federal judge found the outlets are likely to win their First Amendment claim after the White House pulled their access, initially citing "negative coverage" and later reframing it as a national security concern the judge did not accept. The administration has not complied with the order. Both sides have two weeks to file further arguments on a longer-term ruling. (NPR, Reuters)

**5. James Copenhaver, wounded in the 2024 Trump rally shooting, has died at 76.** He was shot in the arm and abdomen at the Butler, Pennsylvania rally that killed Corey Comperatore and wounded David Dutch. Pennsylvania's attorney general said he died of complications from those wounds. Copenhaver and his wife sued the federal government in June alleging Secret Service negligence. (AP, CBS)

> *James 4:14: life is "a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes." Copenhaver survived the bullet. He didn't survive the years after it.*

**6. FDA nominee Overton won't say if Trump was wrong about the measles vaccine.** Dr. Heidi Overton, 37, called the MMR vaccine "safe and effective" but repeatedly declined to say whether Trump was wrong to call it "lethal." She also would not say whether she personally believes mifepristone is safe, despite the FDA's own determination that it is, and despite a 2023 paper she wrote calling chemical abortion a serious risk to women. She said she would "follow the law" regardless of White House pressure. (The Hill)

## Local and California

**7. Judge mostly upholds block on Chino Valley Unified's gender notification policy.** San Bernardino County Superior Court Judge Michael Sachs rejected the district's request to lift his injunction, ruling it doesn't conflict with the Supreme Court's Mirabelli v. Bonta decision as the district argued. His September 15 ruling: schools have no duty to proactively notify parents when a student asks to go by a different name or pronoun, but if a parent directly asks, the school must answer honestly. The requirement to notify parents of changes to official records stays in place. Board President Sonja Shaw says staff will keep notifying parents on any requested record changes. Faithful people land on both sides of this: one side reads it as protecting a vulnerable kid's disclosure on their own terms, the other as the state inserting itself between parents and children on something parents have a right to know. (ABC7)

**8. The Shoppes at Chino Hills sold for $157 million, rebranding as "The Shops."** Stockdale Capital Partners and Hamilton Lane bought the Trader Joe's- and Barnes & Noble-anchored center, the priciest Inland Empire retail deal this year, and plan to remerchandise it with more national retailers. Some residents are pushing back on losing the "Shoppes" name. (Champion, unrated local)
