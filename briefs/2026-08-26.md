# Daily Brief — Wednesday, August 26, 2026

US pauses Iran strikes for a sanctions squeeze, Canada hits back with $20 billion in tariffs. Ohio's proof of citizenship voting rule blocked days before the registration deadline. Chino Hills sales tax measure is locked in for the November ballot.

## International

**1. US pivots from Iran strikes to economic pressure.** Secretary of State Marco Rubio told allies the US does not expect to launch new strikes on Iran "for the time being," shifting the pressure campaign to sanctions under what the administration calls "Operation Economic Outcast." More than 60 people, companies, and vessels tied to Iranian oil revenue and weapons programs have been sanctioned. Trump says the Navy has cleared mines from the main shipping lane in the Strait of Hormuz and warned any new mines will be destroyed on sight. One US official told Axios the pause is expected to hold at least until after the November midterms, after which renewed strikes are back on the table. (Axios, Reuters)

> *"Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain?" (Ps 2:1). The psalm does not answer with a forecast. It ends with the One enthroned unshaken and safety for those who take refuge in him, whatever the next move out of Washington or Tehran turns out to be.*

**2. Indonesia's worst fire season in over a decade.** Fire hotspots across Sumatra and Kalimantan have topped 3,500 this month, more than four times July's total and the highest August count since 2015. Choking haze has spread into Malaysia. Authorities have charged 72 people over the fires and are running aerial water drops across six provinces. (NPR, Bloomberg)

**3. Canada retaliates with tariffs on $20 billion of US goods.** Ottawa matched Washington dollar for dollar after trade talks collapsed, imposing 15 to 50 percent duties on steel, aluminum, dairy, appliances, and electronics effective September 8. Canada is pairing the move with a $7.5 billion aid package for exposed businesses and workers. The tariffs answer Trump's own 50 percent duties on $20 billion of Canadian goods, which took effect Saturday. (AP)

## National

**4. Ohio's citizenship-proof voting rule blocked before the registration deadline.** A federal judge in Cleveland issued a preliminary injunction against Ohio's requirement that voters show proof of citizenship when registering at motor vehicle offices, ruling it likely conflicts with the National Voter Registration Act. The injunction runs through the October 5 registration deadline for the midterms. Ohio's secretary of state says he will appeal immediately. (Reuters, AP)

**5. Meta's child-safety trial opened in Oakland.** Attorneys general from California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey are trying Meta in federal court over claims the company built addictive features, like infinite scroll and autoplay, that it knew would harm kids. The trial is expected to run about six weeks with an advisory jury; the judge holds final say on verdict and penalties. Instagram head Adam Mosseri appeared in court Tuesday. (NPR, CNBC)

> *Prov 31:8-9: "Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves, for the rights of all who are destitute." Whatever the jury or judge eventually finds on the facts, that's the standard the trial is trying, however imperfectly, to apply.*

**6. Darline Graham advances in South Carolina Senate runoff.** Graham, appointed to finish her late brother Sen. Lindsey Graham's term and endorsed by Trump, faces Rep. Ralph Norman in Tuesday's GOP runoff. The winner takes on Democrat Annie Andrews in November. (AP)

## Local and California

**7. Chino Hills sales tax measure locked in for November.** The city council voted in July to place a one-cent Transactions and Use Tax on the November 3 ballot, projected to raise $11.5 million a year for the general fund against a projected $4.3 million shortfall next fiscal year. Groceries, rent, utilities, and prescriptions are exempt. (Champion, unrated local)

**8. Chino police push security upgrades at The Preserve's largest apartment complex.** Chief Kevin Mensen said the department fielded 23 child neglect calls and an average of 31 domestic or disturbance calls a month at Homecoming Apartments (1,645 units) through the first half of the year. The property owner has installed 40 security cameras with 150 more coming, tied into the department's real-time crime center. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the Iran pause: it reads less like strategic restraint than political sequencing. Critics argue that holding fire until after the midterms subordinates deterrence to the election calendar, that Iran has already said it won't let Washington dictate "the timing of war and peace," and that a policy premised on Tehran's patience through November is a bet, not a plan. If Iran calculates that a strike now costs it less than waiting out a possible change in Congress, the pause invites the very attack it's meant to avoid. (Washington Post, opinion, lean left)
