# Daily Brief — Sunday, August 2, 2026

Trump ordered a pause on new Iran strikes over the weekend citing progress toward a deal, while Israeli strikes on Gaza continued even after Hamas agreed to disarm. In Washington, the Blanche AG nomination is stuck over an IRS settlement that shields Trump from audits. In Chino Hills, a kidnapping ended in a fatal shootout with deputies.

## International

**1. Trump pauses new Iran strikes, cites deal parameters.** In a Saturday evening Truth Social post, Trump said he will hold off on ordering new strikes on Iran, claiming Mideast allies have reached the parameters of a deal to end the five-month war. The emerging terms would include full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an end to Iran's nuclear threat. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it struck two more tankers in the strait, and Kuwait reported it intercepted Iranian drones entering its airspace. (The Hill, Axios)

**2. Hamas confirms it will disarm, but Gaza strikes continue.** Hamas said Friday it will begin disarming under the framework Trump's Board of Peace announced, which also calls for Israel to end strikes and withdraw from Gaza. Israel has not agreed to halt operations. Intense strikes Friday night and Saturday killed at least four people in central and northern Gaza. (The Hill, NPR)

> *"He makes wars cease to the end of the earth... Be still, and know that I am God" (Ps 46:9-10). The psalm doesn't say when. It says who is still on the throne while the ceasefires collapse and restart.*

## National

**3. Blanche AG nomination stalled over Trump's IRS settlement.** Senators Cornyn and Tillis are withholding votes on Todd Blanche's attorney general confirmation until the DOJ puts in writing that a settlement of Trump's lawsuit against the IRS doesn't shield him and his businesses from audits on returns filed before the deal. Trump said he'd rather temporarily withdraw Blanche's nomination and resubmit it after the two senators leave office than rewrite the settlement. (Forbes)

> *"By justice a king builds up the land, but he who exacts gifts tears it down" (Prov 29:4). A rule for any ruler, in any administration, on either side of this fight.*

**4. Iran-linked hackers hit water systems in at least seven states.** Michigan and Minnesota both confirmed cyberattacks on municipal water systems this week, with roughly 30 Minnesota facilities and nine Michigan systems affected. FBI officials say they have moderate confidence Iran is behind the intrusions; Trump publicly disputed that attribution and blamed state officials for incompetence. No damage or injuries reported. (Bloomberg, The Hill, Forbes)

**5. GDP slows, new tariffs take effect.** Second-quarter GDP grew at a 1.5% annual rate, down from 2.1% in the first quarter. New tariffs of 10% to 12.5%, structured to survive legal challenge after a Supreme Court defeat on the prior tariff authority, took effect Friday. (NPR)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills kidnapping ends in fatal deputy shooting.** Shukur Aikebaer, 60, was shot and kidnapped outside his Chino Hills home July 29 after stepping out to check an electrical panel. Deputies stopped the suspects' car; when the driver, 67-year-old Zhengfeng Bo, opened the trunk and fired on Aikebaer inside it, deputies returned fire and killed him. Aikebaer died at the scene. Bo's brother, Jianquan Bo, is jailed without bail on murder and kidnapping charges. The two suspects knew the victim through a past business relationship; motive is still under investigation. (KTLA)

**7. Chino fireworks stings: 50-plus residents fined $1,000 each.** Police Chief Kevin Mensen sent citations to more than 50 Chino residents for illegal fireworks on July 4, identified through police drone footage. $1,000 is the statutory maximum fine. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest skeptical case on the Iran deal: this ceasefire cycle has been here before, more than once. Trump declared the truce "OVER" in early July, strikes resumed within days, and the underlying memorandum of understanding has stayed vague enough that Washington and Tehran can't agree on what it actually requires. A deal that hinges on Israel also honoring a fragile Lebanon ceasefire, which Hezbollah says it will block "by any means necessary," is not obviously more durable than the ones that already broke. Read as a pattern rather than a headline, this looks less like resolution and more like the same cycle running again. (Washington Post news analysis)
