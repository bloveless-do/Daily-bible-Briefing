# Daily Brief — Sunday, August 23, 2026

US hits Canada with 50% tariffs, Carney vows dollar-for-dollar retaliation starting Sept. 8. A federal judge struck down Trump's visa ban on 75 countries. A magnitude 5.9 earthquake rattled Tokyo before dawn, injuring dozens.

## International

**1. US-Canada trade talks collapse, 50% tariffs take effect.** Trump's deadline expired Saturday with no deal, and the US imposed 50 percent tariffs on roughly $20 billion in Canadian goods. Prime Minister Mark Carney called the move "a miscalculation" and said Canada will match the tariffs dollar for dollar starting Sept. 8, targeting US steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics. Canada has already imposed a 25 percent tariff on non-USMCA-compliant US auto imports. (Bloomberg, NPR)

**2. Magnitude 5.9 earthquake rattles Tokyo before dawn.** The quake struck at 2 a.m. Sunday, centered in southern Ibaraki Prefecture at a depth of about 70 kilometers. At least 37 people were injured, one seriously. A water main burst in Tokyo's Koto ward and about 460 homes lost power before it was restored. No tsunami threat was issued. (AP)

**3. Kazakhstan holds first elections for new unicameral legislature.** Voters chose among 545 candidates from seven parties for the new 145-seat Kurultai, a restructuring of the country's parliament. (Reuters)

## National

**4. Federal judge strikes down Trump's 75-country visa ban.** U.S. District Judge Jeannette Vargas ruled that Secretary of State Marco Rubio exceeded his statutory authority when the State Department halted immigrant visa processing in January for nationals of 75 countries, selected by a public-assistance-use threshold. The 61-page ruling found the policy "contrary to law" and outside Rubio's authority under the Immigration and Nationality Act. The pause had covered close to 40 percent of the world's countries, including Egypt, Afghanistan, Haiti, Iran, Brazil, and Russia. (NPR, The Hill)

**5. Family of man shot by federal agent during mental health crisis pushes for answers.** Jonah Neal's family and attorney were shown video from the day a federal agent shot him while he was in a mental health crisis. Details on the agency involved and the justification for the shooting remain contested. (AP)

## Local and California

**6. Becerra holds wide lead over Hilton in governor's race.** A UC Berkeley IGS/LA Times poll has Democrat Xavier Becerra at 55 percent to Republican Steve Hilton's 37 percent, with 7 percent undecided, less than three months from the November election. Hilton carries Trump's endorsement; California has not elected a Republican governor since 2003. (ABC7)

**7. Chino American Legion Post 299 set to lose event parking to new development.** A developer is closing by year's end on a lot at Central and Schaefer avenues to build a 7-Eleven, gas station, and car wash, eliminating most of the 68-space overflow lot the Legion hall uses for weddings and quinceañeras. Construction is expected to start early next year. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the tariff approach: economists and Democratic critics argue a 50 percent tariff on a close ally is inflationary for US consumers, hits manufacturers that depend on Canadian steel and potash, and invites a retaliation cycle that lands on Midwest farm exports. Carney's refusal to fold, and his dollar-for-dollar pledge, signal Canada intends to absorb the pain rather than concede, meaning the costs land on both countries' consumers with no clear off-ramp. Critics call it erratic dealmaking rather than a coherent trade strategy. (Bloomberg, NPR)
