Houthi forces seized Yemen's entire Red Sea coast over the weekend as the US-Iran war grinds toward its sixth month and Brent crude tops $107. ## International

**1. Houthis complete takeover of Yemen's Red Sea coast, control Bab al-Mandeb strait.** Iran-backed Houthi forces seized the port city of Mokha and the island of Perim over the weekend, completing their hold on Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline and the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a global shipping chokepoint. Pro-government forces withdrew to regroup after taking casualties. Two Iranian sources told Reuters that Tehran directed the assault and promised the Houthis more weapons and funding, despite publicly denying control of the group. (NPR, Reuters)

**2. Strait of Hormuz still choked, oil past $107 a barrel.** Tanker traffic through Hormuz remains far below prewar levels as attacks continue nearly six months into the US-Iran war. Brent crude has climbed roughly 19% over the past month. Trump said the US could stay in Iran and "keep the oil," and predicted gas prices will fall sharply once the war ends. (Bloomberg, Axios)

## National

**3. AI stocks plunge after Amodei calls for a development slowdown.** Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay Saturday arguing the industry must pace capability gains to match its safety work, warning AI could be capable within six to twelve months of serious autonomous risks. OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk both said they agreed. Monday's selloff hit chipmakers and cloud providers worldwide: Nvidia fell 3%, Arm and Marvell fell 6%, SoftBank fell over 10% in Tokyo. (Bloomberg)

**4. Trump ties $5,000 checks to GOP holding Congress.** Trump said the dividend checks will "happen 100 percent" if Republicans keep their majority after the midterms. The plan would cost an estimated $1.2 trillion and legal analysts say it likely requires an act of Congress, meaning the president can't do it unilaterally even if the GOP wins. (The Hill)

**5. First deportation under secretive Alien Terrorist Removal Court.** A federal judge ordered the removal of Nazira Haji Zada, a lawful permanent resident the government accused of helping her son and son-in-law's foiled 2024 shooting plot. It's the first case run through the Alien Terrorist Removal Court, which relies on classified evidence. Zada accepted the designation and waived her appeal. Her public defenders say the process itself violates due process: neither she nor her attorneys were shown the evidence used against her. (Axios, NPR)

## Local and California

**6. Chino rejects developers' bid to skip undergrounding power lines.** The City Council denied appeals from Warmington Homes, on behalf of Regent Land Company and BCT Development, seeking to leave overhead power lines in place at two affordable housing projects on Riverside Drive rather than bury them, citing cost. The assistant city engineer cited 23 collisions between 2021 and 2025 in which vehicles struck Southern California Edison poles in Chino. The city attorney noted undergrounding has been required by city code since 2002. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. Chino Hills sales tax measure headed to the November ballot.** The City Council placed Measure J, a 1-cent local sales tax, on the Nov. 3 ballot. It's projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for local services and infrastructure, with annual audits and public spending reports built in. (Champion, unrated local)
