# Daily Brief — Saturday, August 15, 2026

Trump says he will declare the Strait of Hormuz a US territory once Iran is "defeated." A Nevada judge threw out the fake-elector case against six Republicans. Chino Hills kidnapping-murder suspect pleads not guilty; sales tax ballot arguments are due Monday.

## International

**1. Trump says he'll declare the Strait of Hormuz US territory.** Speaking to a law enforcement crowd in New York on Friday, Trump said "after we finish defeating Iran, which is being very badly defeated, pretty soon I'll be declaring the Hormuz Strait a territory of the United States." He touted the US naval blockade already in place, saying "no ships get through unless we want them to." He gave no legal mechanism for the claim. Legal scholars note international law protects free transit through the strait regardless of any US declaration, and former diplomat Richard Haass said flatly that no country controls Hormuz. Iran's deputy foreign minister called the idea "delusions," saying the strait "cannot be seized by tweet, nor by aircraft carrier." About 20 percent of global oil supply moves through the waterway, and the Pentagon separately gave defense contractors 21 days to speed up production after the war drained Patriot interceptor stocks by roughly 65 percent. (The Hill, NewsNation)

**2. US ambassador calls West Bank settlers "terrorists" after days-long siege.** Israeli settlers surrounded Palestinian homes in the village of Qusra for days, cutting water and electricity to two families, one of which was sheltering five Italian visitors. Israeli troops moved in Thursday and dismantled two illegal outposts. US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee, a longtime ally of the settler movement, called the besieging settlers "terrorists," an unusual break from his typical posture. The UN has tracked roughly 190 settler attacks a month in the West Bank this year, on pace to exceed 2,000 by year's end. (NPR)

## National

**3. Nevada judge dismisses fake-elector case against six Republicans.** Clark County Judge Mary Kay Holthus ruled Thursday that prosecutors lacked sufficient evidence the six, including Nevada GOP chairman Michael McDonald, intended to defraud anyone when they signed a false 2020 elector certificate for Trump. Holthus noted the six publicly announced what they were doing, which cuts against a deception claim. Nevada Attorney General Aaron Ford's office says it will appeal to the state Supreme Court. Similar cases have now collapsed in Arizona, Michigan, and Georgia; a case is still active in Wisconsin. (The Hill, NPR)

**4. Judge dismisses Trump administration's antisemitism lawsuit against Harvard.** US District Judge Richard Stearns ruled the administration failed to prove an ongoing civil rights violation, finding the incidents cited mostly dated to the 2023-24 school year and were "too isolated and episodic" to sustain the claim. The suit had sought to claw back billions in federal research grants under Title VI. The Justice Department says it is weighing next steps. (NPR)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills kidnapping-murder suspect pleads not guilty.** Jianquan Bo, 66, pleaded not guilty Tuesday to murder with a kidnapping special-circumstance allegation. Prosecutors say Bo and his brother Zhengfeng Bo kidnapped 60-year-old Shukur Aikebaer outside his Monteverde Drive home on July 29 and shot him inside a car trunk; a responding deputy shot and killed Zhengfeng Bo at the scene. Jianquan Bo remains in custody without bail. (KTLA)

**6. Chino Hills sales tax ballot arguments due Monday.** The city council voted 5-0 to place a one-cent Transactions and Use Tax on the Nov. 3 ballot, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for the general fund. Formal arguments for and against must reach the city clerk by 11:30 a.m. Monday. Letters to the editor already show organized opposition, with critics pointing to a $2.5 million budget shortfall last year despite an earlier tax increase and arguing the city should fix its spending before asking for more. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against Trump's Hormuz claim: it has no legal basis under international maritime law, which guarantees passage rights regardless of any unilateral US declaration, and the administration has offered no mechanism for how a territorial claim would even work. Critics argue the rhetoric prolongs uncertainty rather than resolving it, that Trump has pivoted from touting an imminent peace deal to a "wait Iran out" posture that keeps oil markets volatile and the war's endpoint undefined, and that a symbolic land claim distracts from the harder question of what actually ends the conflict. (Bloomberg, NPR)
