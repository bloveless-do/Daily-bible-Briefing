## International

**1. Witkoff and Kushner meet Putin, no breakthrough, Kyiv trip next.** U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow Saturday and held a five-hour meeting with Vladimir Putin, their first visit to Kyiv to follow Sunday. Putin aide Yuri Ushakov called the talks "extremely useful, constructive and substantive" but said no compromise was reached on territory. The Russian side received four documents, including a 27-point plan. Putin ordered no strikes on Kyiv for three nights starting Saturday. Ukrainian officials remain skeptical of Russian intentions. (Reuters, NBC News)

**3. Germany's AfD poised for a historic win Sunday.** Voters in Saxony-Anhalt go to the polls Sunday, with AfD candidate Ulrich Siegmund polling above 40 percent. A win would make AfD the first far-right party to lead a German state government since World War II. Germany's domestic intelligence service classifies the party as far-right extremist in that state. (NPR, CBS News)

## National

**4. Mail-in voting fight continues after Friday injunction.** The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 last month to let the Trump administration proceed with parts of its mail-in voting order, including DHS citizenship-list sharing with states and USPS ballot-design rulemaking, without ruling on the most contested piece: requiring USPS to withhold ballots from voters not on a state-supplied list. A federal judge issued a preliminary injunction Friday night blocking that envelope and portal registration provision, citing disenfranchisement risk 70 days before the midterms. Democratic governors and attorneys general say more suits are coming. (NPR, SCOTUSblog, ABC News)

**5. Data centers become a bipartisan campaign target.** Campaigns and outside groups have spent more than $45 million on ads mentioning data centers this cycle, over 99 percent of it opposing AI infrastructure. Both parties argue the facilities get outsized tax breaks and drive up electricity bills. In Ohio, Democrat Sherrod Brown has run ads branding GOP Sen. Jon Husted "the face of data centers." In Texas, Republican Gov. Greg Abbott, once a booster, now pushes restrictions in rural areas. (Axios, NPR)

**6. Gloria Steinem dies at 92.** The feminist activist and Ms. magazine co-founder died Wednesday in New York City. Tributes have followed from across politics and culture. (AP, NPR)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against how the Moscow trip has been run: Ukrainian officials tell Axios they remain highly skeptical of Putin's intentions and hold intelligence suggesting Russia could be preparing a major escalation rather than a deal. Critics, including voices inside Trump's own administration, argue Witkoff and Kushner have been too deferential to Putin, too quick to credit his sincerity, and too willing to press Ukraine toward ceding territory it currently controls as the price of peace. On this read, meeting Putin at all lends his position credibility, and the three-night pause on striking Kyiv looks less like goodwill than choreography timed to the envoys' visit. (Axios)
