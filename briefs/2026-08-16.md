## International

**1. Strait of Hormuz tensions rise, shipping traffic thins out.** A vessel tied to the UAE's state oil company was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz Friday night. The UAE blamed Iran and called it a flagrant violation of international law; the crew was unharmed. Traffic through the strait fell from roughly 11 ships a day to six over the weekend as US-Iran talks over reopening it drag on. Separately, a Houthi strike on a commercial ship off Yemen's coast killed four crew members. (NPR, The Hill)

**2. Ukraine's deepest strike yet hits a Russian refinery.** Ukrainian long-range drones struck the Taneco oil refinery in Nizhnekamsk, Tatarstan, about 1,200 kilometers inside Russia. Regional officials say at least 13 people died and 75 were injured, the deadliest strike inside Russia in more than two years. Ukraine has hit Russian refineries almost daily in recent months, straining the country's fuel supply. (Washington Times)

## National

**3. DOJ warned it faces contempt over Epstein files.** Attorney General Todd Blanche appeared before US District Judge Emmet Sullivan on Thursday in a lawsuit demanding full release of the Epstein files under the 2025 Transparency Act. Sullivan said he has held DOJ lawyers in contempt before over withheld documents and would again if the department keeps defying release orders. The DOJ says it is still withholding an unspecified number of files to protect victims' privacy. A separate review found the department's own redactions on already-released files were inconsistent, in some cases leaving victims' names and personal information exposed. (Forbes, NewsNation)

**4. New forced-labor tariffs take hold on 60 countries.** The Trump administration's Section 301 tariffs on trading partners over forced-labor import rules took effect this summer. Duties run 10 percent for 17 countries that adopted stronger bans and 12.5 percent for the rest, including China, Vietnam, Brazil, and Thailand, covering nearly all US imports from the targeted economies. (The Hill)
