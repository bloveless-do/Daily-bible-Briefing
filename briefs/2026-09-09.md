# Daily Brief — Wednesday, September 9, 2026

The US destroyed five more Iranian oil tankers and Iran fired missiles at a Jordanian base hosting US forces; no casualties on either side of that exchange. Trump's ban on Canadian dairy, alcohol, and motorcycle imports takes effect September 29. Chino Valley sits under an extreme heat warning through Thursday.

## International

**1. US strikes five more Iranian tankers, Iran hits a Jordan base.** The US military destroyed five Iranian crude oil tankers Tuesday, the Kivik, Charminar, Horizon 1, and Riesco in the Gulf of Oman, and the Derya near Kharg Island, bringing the total to eight tankers destroyed since Saturday. Central Command says the vessels funded the IRGC and its proxies; crews were told to abandon ship before the strikes. Iran's Revolutionary Guard retaliated with ballistic missiles at Jordan's Al-Azraq base, which hosts US forces. Jordan's air defenses intercepted 18 of 20 missiles; the other two fell in unpopulated areas. No casualties reported. (NPR, NewsNation)

**2. Trump bans more Canadian imports, Canada retaliates.** Trump signed proclamations banning Canadian alcohol, dairy products, whey, and larger motorcycles and mopeds effective September 29, replacing the existing 50% tariffs on those goods under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930. Canada's retaliatory tariffs on more than 700 US goods, including steel, dairy, farm equipment, and electronics, took effect Tuesday. (The Hill)

## National

**3. Midterm season opens with House control in play.** Labor Day marked the informal start of the 2026 midterm campaign. Republicans hold narrow majorities in both chambers; Democrats need a net gain of three House seats, Republicans can afford to lose only two. The Cook Political Report rates 17 of 66 competitive races as true toss-ups, including contests in Arizona, California, Pennsylvania, and Washington. Election Day is November 3. (NPR)

**4. Florida recovers 163 missing children in a five-day operation.** The Florida Department of Law Enforcement's "Operation Statewide Shield" located 163 children, ages 2 months to 17 years, across Florida, six other states, Puerto Rico, and 12 countries. Many showed signs of abuse, neglect, or trafficking. Three arrests so far, more expected. (Florida Dept. of Law Enforcement)

## Local and California

**5. Extreme heat warning covers the Inland Empire through Thursday.** The National Weather Service's extreme heat warning for the San Bernardino and Riverside County valleys runs through 8 p.m. Wednesday, highs 104 to 110. A heat advisory follows immediately through 8 p.m. Thursday, highs upper 90s to 105. Check on elderly neighbors, and don't leave kids or pets in cars. (NWS San Diego)

**6. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads toward a November vote.** Measure J, a 1% local sales tax, is confirmed for the November 3 ballot. The city estimates roughly $11.5 million a year for local services and infrastructure if approved. It would not apply to groceries, rent, utilities, prescriptions, labor, or education. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

Critics note the Iran war is approaching six months with no defined endgame. The Pentagon's own estimate puts direct costs near $37.5 billion, with internal figures including base reconstruction running $80 to $100 billion. Consumers have absorbed roughly $100 billion more in fuel costs, about $763 per household, as diesel hit a record $5.85 a gallon this month. A bipartisan Senate majority, including four Republicans, passed a war powers resolution in June directing troop withdrawal; the House has not forced a floor vote on it. The case: this is a war of choice draining the treasury and dividing Congress with no plan to end it. (Washington Post, NPR, Axios)
