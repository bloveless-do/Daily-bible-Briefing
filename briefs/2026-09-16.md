# Daily Brief — Wednesday, September 16, 2026

NATO shot down a drone over Lithuania as a Russian frigate fired flares at a Danish helicopter. The House voted a third time to rein in Trump's Iran war, with a record seven Republicans defecting. Kosovo's ex-president got 25 years for war crimes. Chino Hills moves a sales tax measure toward the November ballot to cover a budget gap.

## International

**1. NATO shoots down drone over Lithuania, Russian frigate fires flares at Danish helicopter.** An Italian NATO jet downed an explosive-laden drone that crossed into Lithuanian airspace from Belarus, the first such shootdown in the country's history. Separately, a Russian frigate fired two pyrotechnic flares at a Danish military helicopter on a routine patrol over international waters, with no warning given. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas told the European Parliament that Putin has shown no sign of stopping and pushed member states to raise the cost on Moscow, including reviewing Russian tourist visas. (Reuters, NBC News)

**2. Kosovo's ex-president Thaci convicted of war crimes, sentenced to 25 years.** An EU-backed court at the Kosovo Specialist Chambers convicted former president Hashim Thaci of murder, torture, cruel treatment and arbitrary detention tied to the 1998-99 war for independence from Serbia. Three former KLA commanders were sentenced alongside him, from 13 to 25 years. Thaci denies all charges. (AP, NPR)

**3. Iran war grinds into its sixth month with no declared end state.** Trump's original prediction of a war lasting weeks has not held. He said this week more strikes are coming and that the war will end "immediately" after the midterms, while aides have reportedly pushed to keep the conflict quiet, not over, until November. Axios reports Washington may be shifting to a "no war, no peace" posture: periodic strikes without sustained engagement. (Reuters, Axios)

## National

**4. House votes a third time to end the Iran war, seven Republicans defect.** The House passed a Democratic-led war powers resolution 220-204 directing Trump to remove US forces from hostilities with Iran. Three new Republicans crossed over this round (Nancy Mace, Mariannette Miller-Meeks, Zach Nunn), joining four repeat defectors. The resolution isn't binding and Trump would veto it if it ever reached his desk. (CBS News, NPR)

**5. DOJ's secret fight for a journalist's LinkedIn records surfaces in court.** A months-long sealed battle between the Justice Department and tech companies, including LinkedIn, over a reporter's records in a national security leak probe became public during federal court argument. LinkedIn argues the request is overly broad. The case weighs the government's leak-investigation interest against First Amendment source-protection concerns; most details remain sealed. (Washington Post reporting on the related leak task force, TV News Check)

**6. Senate holds confirmation hearing for surgeon general nominee.** The Senate health committee heard from three nominees for top health posts Wednesday, including Dr. Nicole Saphier for surgeon general, Trump's third attempt to fill the post this term. (NPR)

## Local and California

**7. Chino Hills advances one-cent sales tax measure toward November ballot.** The council voted to place a one-cent sales tax measure on the Nov. 3 ballot after staff projected a $4.3 million General Fund deficit for 2026-27 ($59.2 million in revenue against $63.5 million in expenditures). The city has $25.4 million in unrestricted reserves, which officials say will run out in three to five years without new revenue. Mayor Brian Johsz told residents the council can't raise the tax itself; voters decide. (Champion, unrated local)

Critics, including Sen.
