# Daily Brief — Tuesday, July 28, 2026

Trump hosts Zelenskyy and Netanyahu at the White House today, right after Lindsey Graham's funeral in Washington. The Iran strike pause is holding but Tehran denies real talks are underway. Haiti's TPS protections officially ended Monday. Chino Hills' sales tax measure is headed to the November ballot.

## International

**1. US-Iran strike pause holds, but Iran denies real negotiations.** The Pentagon has paused strikes on Iran for several days running, with Trump citing "very good and productive conversations" toward a nuclear framework. Iranian officials publicly reject that any negotiations are underway, calling Trump's comments "psychological warfare." Trump says he will resume strikes quickly if the diplomatic push stalls. (Reuters, Axios)

> *"Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain?" (Ps 2:1). Kings and rulers are still taking their stand and plotting together, exactly the psalm's picture. Its answer isn't a forecast of how Tehran's calculus resolves. It's that the One enthroned is unshaken by the maneuvering. That's the whole claim, no more.*

**2. Zelenskyy meets Trump today to press for continued US support.** Zelenskyy meets Trump this morning to discuss the Russia peace process, an $8 billion Ukraine aid package stalled in the Senate, and proposed new sanctions on Russia. One administration official said the White House view is that "now is the time to end the war." (NPR, Axios)

**3. France evacuates thousands as new heat wave threatens wildfire containment.** French authorities evacuated close to 4,000 people from Atlantic coast tourist sites as a heat wave pushes temperatures past 95°F in southwest France and 100°F in Spain, threatening firefighters' hold on a large wildfire west of Bordeaux. (Reuters)

## National

**4. Lindsey Graham's funeral draws Trump and world leaders to Washington.** The South Carolina senator, who died July 11 of an aortic tear, was honored with a Capitol Rotunda ceremony Tuesday ahead of a funeral at Washington National Cathedral attended by Trump, Netanyahu, and Zelenskyy. (AP)

**5. Haiti's TPS protections ended Monday; deportations could start in September.** Temporary Protected Status for Haitian nationals terminated July 27, with work permits tied to the designation expiring July 24, following a June Supreme Court ruling that let the administration end the protections. Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine, a Republican, said ramping up deportations now is a mistake, telling CBS News "Haiti is hell today." Advocates say enforcement operations could begin as soon as September. (NPR, USCIS)

> *Scripture doesn't resolve this one cleanly, and faithful readers land in different places. Romans 13:1-7 grounds a government's authority to set and enforce its own immigration law; Paul treats civil order itself as a good to be honored. Deuteronomy 10:18-19 and Leviticus 19:33-34 ground the opposite instinct: love the sojourner, "for you were sojourners in Egypt." Both are in the text. Picking one without the other flattens it.*

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to the November ballot.** The City Council voted unanimously July 14 to place a 1% local sales tax on the Nov. 3 ballot, projected to generate about $11.5 million a year in city-controlled revenue if voters approve it. (Champion, unrated local)

**7. Chino Police run Junior Academy for local kids.** Officers led a two-day academy July 21-22 for children ages 10 to 13 covering SWAT, K-9, CSI, and patrol operations. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against how the Iran campaign has been handled: critics note this pause is one of several on-again, off-again halts since strikes began, that it follows internal warnings about running down missile-interceptor stockpiles rather than any confirmed diplomatic breakthrough, and that Iran's flat denial of negotiations suggests the administration is narrating progress that does not exist. On this reading, today's Trump-Netanyahu meeting is less about ending the war than about managing strains between two leaders who differ on when and how to stop, with Netanyahu still pushing to "finish the job" militarily. (Washington Post reporting)
