# Daily Brief — Monday, August 31, 2026

US forces struck Iran again on Larak Island, Iran hit back at two American bases in Jordan overnight. A Russian strike near Kyiv killed 37. Top Pentagon commanders quietly objected to extending the Iran deployment. Chino Hills' sales tax measure now has its opposition argument set for November.

## International

**1. US strikes Iran again, Iran hits US bases in Jordan.** US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz Sunday, the first publicly confirmed US strike on Iran in about a month. CENTCOM said Iran's Revolutionary Guard was preparing to launch rockets carrying sea mines into the strait. Iran's Foreign Ministry reported two killed and two injured. Iran's Revolutionary Guard retaliated with ballistic missiles at two US bases in Jordan, King Hussein and al-Azraq, calling the damage "heavy." Jordan's military said it intercepted eight of the incoming missiles. The war, which began February 28, is now in its seventh month with no declared end state. (Reuters, BBC)

**2. Russian strike near Kyiv kills 37.** A Russian strike on the Bucha district west of Kyiv killed at least 37 people Saturday, one of the deadliest single attacks of the year. Fire from the strike damaged at least 50 residential buildings. It came on the third straight day of intensified Russian air raids on the capital, more than four and a half years into the full-scale invasion. (NPR)

## National

**3. Congress returns to face the September 30 funding deadline.** The House returns to Washington this week after a monthlong recess. Top of the agenda is the Senate-passed continuing resolution to keep the government funded past September 30, the hard deadline when full-year appropriations expire. Two funding lapses already hit this year: a four-day shutdown in late January and a six-week Homeland Security-only shutdown that ran mid-February to the end of April. (The Hill)

**4. Oil jumps, Fed turns more hawkish, jobs report due Friday.** Oil prices rose in early trading Monday as the Iran-Jordan exchange rattled Middle East markets. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh struck a more hawkish tone at Jackson Hole than he had in July, calling elevated prices the Fed's main focus. The August jobs report lands Friday. Economists expect payrolls up about 55,000 after July's unexpected dip. (Bloomberg)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to November with opposition set.** The city council voted unanimously July 14 to place a one-cent sales tax on the Nov. 3 ballot, projected to raise $11.5 million a year against a $4.3 million budget shortfall for fiscal 2026-27. The city clerk's office has selected the opposition argument for the voter guide, filed by resident Bill Shannon, who points to the city's existing federal grants, including $1.6 million for fire reduction and $1 million for storm drains in Los Serranos, as reason the tax isn't needed. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The Washington Post reported that four combatant commanders, the chiefs of Naval Operations, European Command, Southern Command and Pacific Command, filed formal "non-concur" objections to Pentagon orders extending the 50,000-plus troop deployment backing the Iran war. Their case: six months in, the war has drained missile interceptor and long-range precision-munitions stockpiles and is weakening the military's ability to respond to threats elsewhere, from Europe to the Pacific. The Pentagon's spokesman called the leak a "crime" and disputed parts of the report. If the commanders are right, last night's strikes are not a sign the war is under control, they're a sign it is setting its own terms. (Washington Post)
