# Daily Brief — Saturday, August 8, 2026

Trump signed new birthright citizenship orders five weeks after the Supreme Court struck the first one down. The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general on a 50-49 overnight vote. Locally, murder charges are filed in the Chino Hills kidnapping case and the FBI corruption probe into Supervisor Curt Hagman continues.

## International

**1. El Obeid, Sudan under siege by drone strikes, cholera spreading.** Hundreds of thousands of civilians in the besieged city are facing near-daily drone strikes, some days with up to 40 drones overhead at once. Strikes have hit schools, a market, water points, and the main power station. Cholera is spreading through the city, worsened by strikes on medical facilities, and women and girls are reporting high rates of sexual violence. The U.N. has warned of a risk of mass atrocities. (NPR)

**2. Iran moves to ban US and Israeli ships from the Strait of Hormuz.** Iran's parliament is reviewing legislation to bar vessels linked to the US, Israel, and other "hostile countries" from the strait until Tehran is compensated for war damage, and to charge other ships a toll of up to 7% of cargo value. A US official told NPR the strait is international waters that no single party controls. The move comes more than five months into the US-Israel war against Iran that began February 28. Iran is separately finalizing a shipping-routes agreement with Oman. (NPR)

**3. Senate passes Russia and Iran sanctions bill named for the late Sen. Lindsey Graham.** The Senate passed the bill 86-11, renaming it the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act after the South Carolina senator who championed it before his death last month. It authorizes mandatory sanctions on Russian officials including Putin, and tariffs up to 100% on countries, including China and India, that buy Russian oil and gas. The bill now needs a House vote; the House is on recess until September. (NPR, Bloomberg)

## National

**4. Trump signs new birthright citizenship executive orders.** Trump signed two orders Thursday, five weeks after the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that his original 2025 order was unconstitutional. One targets commercial "birth tourism"; the other broadens the list of people the administration considers ineligible for birthright citizenship, including children of some foreign nationals. Legal challenges are expected. (NPR)

**5. Senate confirms Todd Blanche as attorney general, 50-49.** The vote came after 4 a.m. Saturday following a late-night session ahead of the August recess. Every Democrat voted no, along with Republicans Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski. Sen. Bill Cassidy's support Friday, after he'd wavered, provided the deciding vote. Blanche was Trump's former personal defense lawyer. (NPR)

**6. Economy shed 23,000 jobs in July, unemployment fell anyway.** Employers cut 23,000 jobs, missing forecasts of roughly 100,000 in gains, and May-June payrolls were revised down by a combined 103,000. Local public schools cut 50,000 jobs, restaurants and bars 26,000, retailers 19,000. Unemployment dipped to 4.1% only because more than 260,000 people left the workforce. It was the second straight month of a weaker-than-expected jobs report. (NPR, Bloomberg)

## Local and California

**7. FBI corruption probe into Supervisor Curt Hagman continues.** The FBI served sealed search warrants in late July at Hagman's Chino home and his Chino Hills office, part of a public corruption investigation reportedly touching possible foreign influence. Four other locations were searched the same day, including homes tied to a Hagman business partner and an Ontario councilman. No charges have been filed and few details have been released. (Champion, unrated local)

**8. Murder charges filed in Chino Hills kidnapping-killing.** Jianquan Bo, 66, was charged with murder with a kidnapping special circumstance after the July 29 abduction and killing of Shukur Aikebaer, 60, at his Chino Hills home. Aikebaer was shot and placed in a car trunk; the other suspect, Zhengfeng Bo, 67, was shot and killed by deputies after he fatally shot Aikebaer in front of them during a stop. Prosecutors say the case grew out of a business relationship between the men. (ABC7 Inland Empire)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the new birthright citizenship orders: the administration is re-litigating a fight it already lost 6-3 at the Supreme Court five weeks ago, and doing it again anyway signals that constitutional rulings are treated as opening bids rather than settled law. The ACLU has said any new order rewriting birthright citizenship "will meet the same fate as the last one," and state officials, including Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, are already vowing not to comply, calling it unconstitutional. Civil rights attorneys argue the practical effect, regardless of how the litigation ends, is months of fear and legal uncertainty for immigrant families who did nothing but have a baby on US soil. (NPR reporting on reaction)
