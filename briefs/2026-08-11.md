# Daily Brief — Tuesday, August 11, 2026

Netanyahu rejected Trump's Gaza disarmament plan; both sides refuse to move first. A Ukrainian drone strike killed 13 at a Russian oil hub. DC's National Guard deployment hits one year, extended to 2029 at $1.4 billion. Meta's child-safety trial opened in Oakland. Chino Hills put a sales tax measure on the November ballot.

## International

**1. Netanyahu rejects Trump-backed Hamas disarmament plan.** Trump's Board of Peace announced a "historic" agreement in late July for Hamas to disarm in Gaza. Netanyahu rejected the roadmap Aug. 9, demanding Hamas disarm before any Israeli withdrawal begins. Israel had already flagged "serious security concerns" Aug. 2, with its own intelligence assessing Hamas intends to rebuild rather than genuinely demilitarize. Hamas leadership disputes it ever agreed to disarm at all. Trust between the two sides is effectively zero, and each is waiting for the other to move first. (NPR, Axios)

> *"Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain?" (Ps 2:1). The psalm does not end with a forecast. It ends with the One enthroned unshaken by any of it. That is the frame, not a prediction about who blinks first.*

**2. Ukrainian drone strike kills 13 at Russian oil hub.** A Ukrainian drone attack Monday on Nizhnekamsk in Russia's Tatarstan region killed 13 people, including a child, and wounded 75, regional authorities said. The strike hit an oil refinery and is one of the deadliest single attacks of the war. It is part of Ukraine's monthslong campaign against Russian energy infrastructure, with domestically built long-range drones now reaching roughly 1,200 miles into Russian territory. (NPR)

## National

**3. National Guard's DC deployment marks one year, extended to 2029.** A Pentagon estimate puts the cost of extending the deployment through January 2029 at roughly $1.4 billion, based on an average of 2,500 Guard members remaining in the capital through fiscal 2027-29. The administration frames the deployment as a crime-reduction measure; local officials and congressional Democrats call it a costly, open-ended federal intrusion into city governance. Guardsmen have also taken on civic tasks like trash pickup and graffiti removal. (NPR, The Hill)

**4. Meta's child-safety trial opens in Oakland.** A Ninth Circuit panel denied Meta's emergency bid to pause the federal trial, which opened this week with the first four of 29 states suing over claims Meta knowingly designed Instagram and Facebook features to addict children. Meta denies the design was intentional and disputes that social media addiction is an established psychiatric condition. The trial follows a New Mexico court's Aug. 7 order that Meta pay $567 million over child mental-health harms, with $420 million earmarked for treatment services. (Bloomberg, NPR)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills puts one-cent sales tax on November ballot.** The City Council voted unanimously to place a Chino Hills Services Measure, a one-cent Transactions and Use Tax, on the Nov. 3 ballot. City estimates put annual revenue at $11.5 million. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Chino Hills kidnapping-murder suspect pleads not guilty.** Jianquan Bo, 66, pleaded not guilty Aug. 4 to murder with a kidnapping special-circumstance allegation in the death of Shakur Aikebaer, 60. Co-defendant Zhengfeng Bo was shot and killed by deputies at the scene during the abduction. Bo's next court date is Aug. 12, with a preliminary hearing set for Aug. 17. (ABC7, Inland Empire desk)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case that Trump's "historic" Gaza deal is more announcement than agreement: neither side has actually moved. Israel's own intelligence assesses Hamas plans to rebuild rather than disarm, and Hamas's leadership says no one even raised full disarmament with them directly. Each side is conditioning its own next step on the other side going first, which is the same standoff that has stalled every prior version of this plan. The White House's framing has consistently run ahead of what has actually been secured on the ground. (Washington Post)
