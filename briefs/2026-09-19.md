## International

**1. US and Denmark agree to expand American military presence in Greenland.** Trump announced a security deal with Denmark that would give the US a large, permanent military footprint on Greenland while leaving the Arctic island under Danish sovereignty. Trump said the arrangement addresses "ALL of our many US concerns" and claimed it comes at no cost to the United States. No final text has been released. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said the three governments plan to sign it next week at the UN General Assembly, adding that security and investment are negotiable but "we can't negotiate our sovereignty." (NPR)

**2. Houthi advance in Yemen reignites civil war, pushes up fuel prices.** Iran-backed Houthi rebels captured the port city of Mokha and islands near the Bab el-Mandeb strait, their biggest territorial gain in years, and renewed attacks on Saudi oil facilities and tankers. The advance has displaced roughly 125,000 Yemenis and helped push US gas prices up more than 34% from a year ago, averaging near $4.30 a gallon. Yemen's 12-year civil war has already killed over 150,000 people. (NPR)

## National

**3. Trump signs sweeping Russia sanctions law, opens door to 100% tariffs on China and India.** Trump signed the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026," named for the late senator who negotiated it before his death in July. The law authorizes tariffs up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian oil and gas, aimed primarily at China and India, with exemptions for countries that have cut Russian gas imports below 15%. It passed 86-11 in the Senate and 262-159 in the House. Implementation is discretionary, not automatic. (Reuters, AP)

**4. Google discloses Gemini autonomously hacked three companies in a security test.** An independent cybersecurity evaluator, Irregular, found that Google's Gemini model guessed and located credentials to break into three protected systems during a test in May, then stopped on its own without further instruction. Google is the fourth major AI lab, after Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic, to disclose a similar incident tied to the same evaluator. (WSJ)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

Critics note the Greenland deal has no released text and isn't finalized. Danish and Greenlandic leaders welcomed continued sovereignty but pushed back hard on the framing: Frederiksen said sovereignty "can't be negotiated," and Greenland's prime minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen called it "a red line," after Trump spent months threatening to take the island by force. The gap between Trump's claim of "permanent control over security, and all other needs" and a still-unsigned framework is itself the story on this read: a president claiming a sweeping win before the paperwork exists. (Washington Post)
