# Daily Brief — Thursday, September 10, 2026

Iran-US tanker war escalates and IAEA refers Iran to the UN Security Council. Trump pledges a $5,000 "dividend" if the GOP holds Congress. Chino's business license tax measure heads to the November ballot, and an extreme heat warning covers the valley through tonight.

## International

**1. Iran war escalates: tanker strikes, Jordan air base hit, IAEA refers Iran to UN Security Council.** US forces sank five Iranian oil tankers Sept. 8 after Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at a US Navy warship twice in two days. Iran retaliated, striking eight tankers and two American vessels near the Strait of Hormuz and hitting Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan with ballistic missiles. One A-10 was left with a damaged wing and roughly eight F-15s took light damage; no US personnel were killed. Separately, the IAEA's 35-nation board voted 23-3-8 to refer Iran to the UN Security Council for breaching nonproliferation obligations, the first such referral in 20 years. Russia and China, Iran's allies, hold Security Council vetoes, so the move changes little on the ground. (NPR, Axios)

**2. UK, France, Canada sanction Israeli West Bank settlements; Israel closes British consulate.** The UK banned imports of goods from Israeli settlements in the West Bank and barred UK companies from financing, building, or advertising them, with Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband accusing settlers of "ethnic cleansing." France and Canada said they'll follow with similar measures; twelve countries total signed a joint statement backing a two-state solution. Israel called the move outrageous, announced it will close the British consulate in Jerusalem, and will bar 12 UK members of Parliament from entering the country. (AP)

## National

**3. Trump pledges $5,000 "dividend" to every adult if GOP holds Congress.** At the RNC's first-ever midterm convention in Dallas, Trump said Republicans would issue a $5,000 "Trump Dividend" check to every American adult if the party keeps the House and Senate in November, comparing it to a company's shareholder payout. The White House has not said how it would be funded or pushed through Congress. Back-of-envelope math on roughly 240 million adults puts the cost above $1 trillion; VP Vance floated tariff revenue, which runs far short of that sum. (NPR)

**4. NTSB: pilot warned Amazon cargo plane was coming in too fast before fatal Miami crash.** Cockpit voice recordings released this week show one pilot repeatedly warned, starting nearly two minutes before touchdown, that the plane was too fast, then urged an aborted landing at the last second. The other pilot never gave a clear response. The plane overran the runway by 1,300 feet, hit a van and an SUV, and caught fire, killing five people who were in the van. Flight data shows the crew briefly tried to abort and retry the landing before giving up on it four seconds later. (NPR)

## Local and California

**5. Chino's Measure F business license tax reform heads to Nov. 3 ballot.** The city council placed a measure on the ballot that would replace Chino's roughly 40-year-old flat business license tax structure with a tiered one, projected to raise about $4.8 million a year for city services. Businesses with $75,000 or less in gross receipts (about 56% of active Chino businesses) would pay a flat $25; above that, rates run $0.60 to $1.50 per $1,000 in receipts. The city says it does not tax residents directly. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Extreme heat warning covers the Inland Empire through tonight.** The National Weather Service's San Diego office issued an extreme heat warning for the San Bernardino and Riverside County valleys, including Chino Valley, in effect until 8 p.m. today, with highs in the upper 90s to 108 and tropical-level humidity layered on top. (KTLA)
