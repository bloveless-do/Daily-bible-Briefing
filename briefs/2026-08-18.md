# Daily Brief — Tuesday, August 18, 2026

Trump threatens to bomb Oman and claims the US controls the Strait of Hormuz as the Iran deadline expires. Ukraine hits Moscow with its largest drone barrage in two years. DOJ opens a fentanyl watchdog probe. Chino Hills sales tax heads to the ballot.

## International

**1. Trump threatens Oman, claims US "controls" the Strait of Hormuz.** The 60-day US-Iran memorandum expired Monday with no deal. Trump told Fox News Iran should "put up the white flag of surrender" and threatened to "bomb the s--- out of" Oman if it "gets in the way," after Oman brokered a shipping arrangement with Tehran to ease the standoff. Trump also claimed the US already controls the strait through its blockade and floated declaring it US territory. The same weekend he ordered the Pentagon to substantially scale back joint military exercises with South Korea, citing cost and Seoul's refusal to join the Iran war. (Bloomberg, The Hill)

**2. Ukraine launches largest drone barrage on Moscow in two years; Russian strike kills 10 in Ukrainian village.** Ukraine fired at least 637 drones toward Moscow overnight Monday into Tuesday, the second major salvo in two days. Russian air defenses claimed 197 intercepts. A Wildberries logistics warehouse took damage and more than 5,000 customers in the Moscow region lost power. Separately, a Russian missile strike on a village in Ukraine's Kharkiv region killed at least 10 people and wounded eight. (AP)

## National

**3. DOJ inspector general opens review of DEA fentanyl "controlled delivery" tactic.** New DOJ Inspector General Don Berthiaume announced a nationwide review Monday, one of his first acts since being sworn in Friday, into DEA policies that let agents monitor but not seize fentanyl shipments to protect larger investigations. It follows an AP investigation that found DEA agents in New Mexico repeatedly tracked but did not stop major shipments between 2023 and 2025, including a 74,000-pill delivery to an Albuquerque mobile home park. Two more whistleblowers have since come forward. (AP)

> *"Rescue those being led away to death... If you say, 'Behold, we did not know this,' does not he who weighs the heart perceive it?" (Prov 24:11-12). That is the charge against a policy of letting a shipment walk to protect a case file.*

**4. Congressional Democrats hold field hearing on fatal ICE shooting in Biddeford, Maine.** Rep. Chellie Pingree led a Monday session with Reps. Bennie Thompson, Katherine Clark, and Troy Carter examining the July 13 shooting of Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero, 25, a Colombian national killed during an ICE operation. He was not the intended target. His roommate, the actual target and owner of the vehicle Durán Guerrero was driving, also lacked a final removal order. DHS says the agent fired fearing for public safety after the vehicle tried to flee. (NPR, The Hill)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills places one-cent sales tax on the November ballot.** The city council voted unanimously July 14 to put a Transactions and Use Tax measure before voters Nov. 3. It would raise an estimated $11.5 million a year for the general fund against a projected $4.3 million shortfall in FY 2026-27. Groceries, rent, utilities, education, and prescriptions are exempt. Revenue would stay under local control. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Murder charges filed in Chino Hills kidnapping-killing; suspect pleads not guilty.** San Bernardino County prosecutors charged Jianquan Bo, 66, with murder and kidnapping Aug. 3 over the July 29 killing of Shukur Aikebaer, 60, calling it a calculated, targeted attack in which power was cut to the victim's home before he was abducted and shot in a car trunk. A special circumstance allegation makes Bo eligible for life without parole or the death penalty. A second suspect, Zhengfeng Bo, 67, was shot and killed by deputies at the scene. Jianquan Bo pleaded not guilty in a Rancho Cucamonga courtroom. (KTLA, ABC7)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

Oman's foreign minister, Badr Albusaidi, has called the Iran war Trump's "greatest miscalculation" and said flatly "this is not America's war," criticizing Washington for backing Israel into the conflict. Analysts argue that threatening to bomb a 50-year regional partner that was actively brokering a peaceful shipping arrangement, on the same weekend the administration alienated South Korea, is not strength but incoherence: it burns goodwill with the allies doing the actual work of de-escalation, spooks oil markets already jumpy over the strait, and does it heading into midterms where the war is unpopular at home.
