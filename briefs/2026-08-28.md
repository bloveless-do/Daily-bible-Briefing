# Daily Brief — Friday, August 28, 2026

Hormuz shipping lanes declared mine-free as Iran tensions ease. Autopsy contradicts official account of a Memphis shooting by Tennessee National Guard troops. Trump signs an order renaming Lake Ontario "Lake America," escalating the Canada trade war. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to the ballot.

## International

**1. US declares Strait of Hormuz mine-free, Trump says "that sucker is open."** CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said internationally recognized sea routes through the strait are now clear of Iranian mines, calling it a major milestone. Trump told Axios Iran's response has been "very mild" and that's "the whole ball game." Shipping data is mixed: US officials cite 20-30 tankers moving nightly, roughly half of pre-war volume, while Reuters preliminary data shows daily transits swinging from 17 down to 7 against a 10-day average of 15. (Reuters, Axios)

**2. Congo's Ebola outbreak spreads to two more health zones, now 60 total.** The fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in history has reached 5,794 confirmed cases and 2,786 deaths in eastern Congo's North Kivu province, per government figures released Friday. WHO says it's on track to surpass the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak, the deadliest on record. Spread is driven by insecurity, displacement, and a health workers' strike slowing the response. (AP)

> *"His mercies... are new every morning" (Lam 3:22-23). Written from inside a siege, not after it lifted. Worth sitting with before moving to the next story.*

**3. US closes in on "massive" Venezuela oil deal.** Axios reports the administration is negotiating an ownership stake in more than a dozen Venezuelan oil fields, a deal officials describe as potentially doubling US oil reserves. The push is driven partly by the Strategic Petroleum Reserve sitting at a 40-year low after disruptions from the Iran and Ukraine wars. (Axios)

## National

**4. Trump signs order renaming Lake Ontario "Lake America," Canada trade war deepens.** The executive order directs Interior Secretary Doug Burgum to rename the lake within 30 days for federal use, following a breakdown in US-Canada trade talks and new retaliatory tariffs. Trump says Canada has been "ripping us off" on trade. The order can't compel Canada or international bodies to adopt the name. Canadian officials, including trade minister Dominic LeBlanc, have said they won't respond to the administration's social media posts on it. (Reuters)

**5. Autopsy contradicts official account of Tennessee National Guard shooting in Memphis.** A Shelby County Medical Examiner autopsy shows 20-year-old Tyrin Johnson was shot once in the back, the bullet exiting his upper right chest at a slightly upward angle, consistent with him running away. Guard officials had said he "rounded on them and pointed a tan handgun" before two troops fired. The medical examiner ruled the death a homicide. (AP)

> *"Let justice roll down like waters" (Amos 5:24). The prophet isn't waiting for a verdict to say that. Neither should this brief. The facts here are contested and unresolved, and both can be true at once.*

**6. Wyoming governor: DOJ election monitors were "a bit aggressive" during primary.** Gov. Mark Gordon (R) has asked the state attorney general to review the conduct of two DOJ attorneys sent to observe Laramie County's primary. County Clerk Debra Lee (R) said the monitors asked for training guides and wanted to examine tabulators, questions she called outside the scope of federal voting rights law. Neither official was told in advance. (The Hill)

## Local and California

**7. Chino Hills places one-cent sales tax measure on the November ballot.** The city council voted to send the measure to voters Nov. 3. (Champion, unrated local)

**8. Lithium-ion battery fire hits Chino warehouse.** Boxes of lithium-ion batteries caught fire early Monday in a shipping warehouse on Ramona Avenue. No one was inside and no injuries were reported; cause remains under investigation. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the Lake Ontario renaming: this is a symbolic, legally toothless gesture that Canada has explicitly said it won't dignify with a response, timed exactly when the administration needs allied cooperation on Iran, Venezuela, and the Strait of Hormuz. Critics argue it converts a trade dispute into a personal feud, costs nothing to Canada in practice, and signals impulsiveness to the same allies being courted for the oil and shipping deals above. (Washington Post reporting)
