## National

**4. Supreme Court allows White House ballroom construction to continue, 5-4.** The conservative majority ruled the National Trust for Historic Preservation likely lacks standing to sue, letting demolition and construction proceed while the underlying case continues in lower courts. Chief Justice Roberts joined the three liberal justices in dissent, writing the construction is "likely unlawful" and that the majority is letting it continue "not because that construction is legal" but on a standing technicality. The ballroom and an underground bunker are now estimated at $600 million, up from an initial $400 million. (NPR, Axios)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the ballroom ruling: this was never really about a room. It's about a president demolishing part of a listed historic building on federal property without congressional appropriation, then getting to keep building because the courts moved too slowly to stop him before the walls came down. Roberts' own dissent makes the point: the majority didn't say the construction was lawful, it said the challenger couldn't prove it was hurt enough to sue. Critics argue that logic lets any administration create facts on the ground and dare the courts to unwind them later, a precedent that outlasts this particular building. (NPR, Washington Post)
