# Daily Brief - Wednesday, September 2, 2026

US struck Iran again after tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. Russia hit Kyiv for a sixth straight night, killing at least 12. The House passed a stopgap funding bill to avoid a shutdown before the midterms. Chino Hills briefed residents on a November sales tax measure.

## International

**1. US strikes Iran again after tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz.** Two supertankers, the Bahri-operated Sidr and the South Korean-operated Senegal Prosperity, were hit by projectiles Aug. 31 inside the US-guarded shipping corridor in the Strait, the first attacks on commercial shipping in that lane. US Central Command said it struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets in response, and in retaliation for an Iranian attack on a US base in Jordan earlier in the week. Trump warned of a harder response if Iran does not stand down. (Bloomberg)

> *"Why do the nations rage?" (Ps 2:1). The psalm does not answer with a timetable. It answers with the One enthroned unshaken and a blessing on those who take refuge in him. Worth reading whole this morning.*

**2. Russia hits Kyiv for a sixth straight night, killing at least 12.** Russia bombarded Kyiv for more than 12 hours overnight into Tuesday with ballistic and cruise missiles, anti-radar missiles, and 218 drones and decoys, an unprecedented sixth consecutive night of heavy strikes on the capital. Six employees of the state railway company died when a missile hit a depot. The strikes wounded more than a dozen, including three children, the same day millions of Ukrainian schoolchildren returned to class. The EU's top diplomat said Russia is bogged down on the ground and trying to scare off Ukraine's Western backers from the air. (AP)

> *Ps 46:1. "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble." Not a claim about how the war ends. A claim about where help actually is while it's still going.*

## National

**3. Kirk assassination suspect will stand trial.** Utah Judge Tony Graf ruled Tuesday that Tyler Robinson will stand trial on all seven counts in the killing of Charlie Kirk, including aggravated murder, after prosecutors presented video, DNA, circumstantial evidence, and what they described as a confession. Graf found the evidence sufficient to support probable cause that Robinson was the shooter. Prosecutors intend to seek the death penalty. (NPR)

> *Rom 13:4. The governing authority "does not bear the sword in vain." A trial, not a mob and not a foregone verdict, is what that verse actually asks for.*

**4. House passes stopgap funding bill, averts shutdown before midterms.** The House voted 370-48 Tuesday to fund the government through Dec. 11, avoiding a shutdown when the fiscal year ends Sept. 30. The bill already passed the Senate and goes to Trump, who is expected to sign it. It blocks a proposed OMB rule that would put federal grant funding under political-appointee review and bars the administration from shifting funds from other programs into Border Patrol. (NPR)

**5. Tropical Storm Edouard swamps the Gulf Coast.** Edouard made landfall near the Texas-Louisiana border Tuesday afternoon with 60 mph winds, gusting to 90 mph in Port Arthur. More than 75,000 lost power in Texas. The storm weakened to a depression by Wednesday morning but continues to dump heavy rain, with up to 8 more inches possible in parts of Texas and Louisiana. (AP)

## Local and California

**6. Chino Hills briefs residents on November sales tax measure.** The city held a briefing Tuesday morning with City Manager Rod Hill on the Chino Hills Services Measure, a one-cent local sales tax headed to the Nov. 3 ballot. If approved, it would raise an estimated $11.5 million a year toward a projected $4.3 million structural budget shortfall for FY 2026-27. The council placed it on the ballot unanimously in July. (Champion, unrated local; chinohills.org)

**7. Chino Planning Commission rejects battery storage project.** The commission voted down Aypa Power's proposed 400-megawatt Dirac battery storage facility near Ayala Park in July, after residents raised safety concerns about the site sitting next to a Southern California Edison substation. Proponents argued it would help the state hit clean-energy targets. (Champion, unrated local; CBS Los Angeles)

The strongest case against the current Iran campaign: a Washington Post poll finds opposition to the strikes has eased since they began, but a steady majority still say the Trump administration has not clearly explained what the war is trying to achieve. Critics argue that six months of intermittent strikes without a stated end state, now expanding into direct hits on commercial shipping lanes, is drift dressed up as strategy, and that neither side shows signs of backing down.
