# Daily Brief — Wednesday, August 5, 2026

Russia hit Kyiv with its heaviest barrage in weeks, killing 17. Todd Blanche's attorney general nomination cleared committee 12-10 after a fight over a scrapped DOJ payout fund. In Chino Hills, murder charges landed in last week's kidnapping case, and the FBI's search of a county supervisor's Chino home is still unexplained.

## International

**1. Russian barrage kills 17 in Kyiv, zero missiles intercepted.** Russia launched 115 drones and 28 missiles, including 24 ballistic missiles, at Kyiv and the surrounding region overnight. Ukraine intercepted 98 of the drones but none of the missiles. President Zelenskyy said the country's supply of Patriot interceptors has fallen to a third of last year's level, blaming a global shortage worsened by the US-Israeli war with Iran and the Trump administration's wind-down of aid to Kyiv. Forty-four people were wounded. (NPR)

**2. Indonesia ferry fire leaves 5 dead, 41 missing.** The Mutiara Sentosa 2, carrying 271 people from Surabaya to Makassar, caught fire early Sunday. Nearby vessels rescued 225 people. Search and rescue for the remaining 41 continues. (NPR)

## National

**3. Blanche's AG nomination advances 12-10, teed up for a floor vote before recess.** The Senate Judiciary Committee voted along party lines Tuesday to send Todd Blanche's nomination to the full Senate. Republicans Cornyn and Tillis had withheld support until the DOJ formally rescinded a proposed $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization fund" that could have compensated people who claimed they were unfairly targeted by the government, including Jan. 6 defendants, and until the scope of a related tax-audit immunity deal for Trump was narrowed to past audits only. Sen. Susan Collins, R-Maine, broke with her party to vote no. All committee Democrats opposed the nomination. (NPR)

**4. Arson suspect charged in Spokane's Old Trails Fire.** Aaron Farinacci, 37, was arrested Monday on suspicion of first-degree arson in the largest of three fires burning around Spokane. A citizen tip and matches, a lighter, and cigarettes found in his fanny pack led to the arrest; he was released after an initial round of questioning before being taken back into custody. The fires have destroyed roughly 700 buildings and forced about 67,000 evacuations. (NPR)

## Local and California

**5. Murder charge filed in Chino Hills kidnapping-killing.** Jianquan Bo, 66, pleaded not guilty in a Rancho Cucamonga courtroom to murder with a special-circumstances kidnapping allegation. Prosecutors say he and his brother, Zhengfeng Bo, cut power to victim Shukur Aikebaer's Chino Hills home on July 29 to lure him outside, shot him, and forced him into a car trunk. Deputies killed Zhengfeng Bo at the end of a short chase. (ABC7, KTLA)

**6. FBI searches San Bernardino County Supervisor Curt Hagman's Chino home.** Agents served warrants at Hagman's Chino residence, a Chino Hills location believed to be his office, and a Beverly Hills property, plus a home in Ontario. The warrants remain sealed and the FBI has not said what prompted the searches. Hagman represents the county's Fourth District. (ABC7, KTLA)
