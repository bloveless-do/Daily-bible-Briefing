# Daily Brief — Tuesday, September 8, 2026

US forces disabled three Iranian oil tankers after Iran fired ballistic missiles at two Navy warships. Canada's retaliatory tariffs on $27.6 billion of US goods took effect today. Chino Hills' Measure J sales tax measure is headed to the November ballot.

## International

**1. US strikes Iranian oil tankers after Iran fires on Navy ships.** Iran's Revolutionary Guard launched ballistic missiles at a US aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer on Saturday. Both evaded the attacks and no American personnel were hurt. CENTCOM retaliated by disabling two Iranian crude tankers, the Downy off Kharg Island and the Stark 1 near Jask, and destroying a third, the Kylo, near the Gulf of Oman, which CENTCOM says fund the IRGC's regional network. Iran's parliament speaker warned that any further US strikes on Iranian assets will be met with retaliation against Gulf energy infrastructure. Saudi Aramco's Jizan refinery, a 400,000-barrel-per-day facility, was hit again Monday, the second strike in a month after an earlier Houthi attack. No group has claimed the latest one. (NBC News, CNBC, ABC News)

## National

**3. Canada's retaliatory tariffs take effect.** Canada's counter-tariffs on US goods, worth $27.6 billion, took effect today after trade talks broke down last month. Rates run 50% on steel, aluminum, furniture and clothing, 25% on dairy and appliances, and 15% on electronics, in response to the 50% US tariffs Washington imposed on Canadian goods August 22. Both governments continue to blame each other for the collapse in talks. (CNBC)

**4. Fed weighs a rate hike ahead of September 16 decision.** Fed Chair Kevin Warsh told the Jackson Hole symposium last week that summer inflation readings, while better than expected, don't show underlying trends have meaningfully improved, and that the bank may have "work to do." Futures markets now put the odds of a quarter-point hike near 56%, up from under 30% before the speech. Trump has continued to push the Fed toward cuts instead. (CNBC, Washington Post)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills Measure J sales tax heads to November ballot.** The city council placed a 1-cent sales tax increase on the November 3 ballot, raising the rate from 7.75% to 8.75% within city limits. Backers project about $11.5 million a year in new revenue, dedicated to local services and infrastructure, with an independent annual audit requirement attached. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. SCE credit lands on September bills.** Southern California Edison residential customers are getting a second $36 credit on September statements, following an August installment, for a combined $72 in relief funded through California's Cap-and-Invest program. SCE says rates are also down about 4.3% on average this year. (KTLA)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case that the Iran campaign has gone wrong: the Washington Post reported that the Army, Navy, and Air Force service chiefs, along with the four-star commanders over Europe, Asia, and Latin America, formally warned Defense Secretary Hegseth in an August 14 order that six months of large-scale Iran operations has pulled ships, aircraft, and personnel away from those theaters and left the US less able to respond to threats elsewhere, including at home. Three of those commands, plus the Navy's top admiral, filed formal non-concurrence with the order to extend anyway, meaning they will carry it out while objecting on the record. The critics' argument: this isn't restraint or resolve, it's a war that outgrew its plan and is now drawing down readiness the country may need somewhere else. (Washington Post reporting)
