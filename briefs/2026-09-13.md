# Daily Brief — Sunday, September 13, 2026

IAEA board reports Iran to the UN Security Council, first time in 20 years. Sweden votes today in a knife-edge election. Trump's Dublin visit draws one of Ireland's largest anti-war protests in years. Chino Hills sales tax measure headed to November ballot.

## International

**1. IAEA reports Iran to UN Security Council.** The agency's 35-nation board voted 23-3 (Russia, China, Niger opposed, 8 abstentions) on Wednesday to report Iran for failing to give inspectors access to nuclear sites and account for its enriched uranium stockpile. It is the first such referral in 20 years, and follows a June 2025 non-compliance finding that preceded Israel's and the US's strikes on Iran's nuclear program. Referral opens the door to possible sanctions, but Russia and China hold Security Council vetoes, so punitive action is unlikely. The Council itself remains split: at a Sept. 10 session, Russia and China challenged the legality of the sanctions "snapback" already in force, and a procedural vote to keep debating the issue passed 11-2. (Reuters, Al Jazeera, UN press office)

**2. Sweden votes in a dead-even election.** Polls closed today in a race exit surveys put at roughly 49-51 between the governing right bloc under Ulf Kristersson and Magdalena Andersson's center-left opposition. The nationalist Sweden Democrats are positioned to become the country's second-largest party for the first time. Preliminary results are due tonight; the certified count takes about a week. (Reuters, NBC News)

**3. Trump's Dublin visit draws mass protest.** Trump met Irish President Catherine Connolly and Taoiseach Micheál Martin in Dublin Saturday, a working visit rather than a state one, before heading to his golf resort in Doonbeg for a tournament running through today. Talks touched on the Iran war, which Irish leaders have raised concerns about. Thousands marched through central Dublin after Trump left for the coast, calling him a warmonger over the Iran conflict, in one of the country's largest anti-war demonstrations in years. (Reuters, CBS News, Washington Post)

## National

**4. Mail-in ballot fight reaches the Supreme Court a third time.** The Trump administration asked the Court again this week to let a new Postal Service rule take effect ahead of the midterms. The rule would require states to submit voter mailing lists to USPS and bars carriers from delivering ballots to voters not on those lists. A coalition of 23 states and DC, led by California, is suing in Boston; Judge Indira Talwani has twice blocked the rule, most recently converting her temporary order into a preliminary injunction. (SCOTUSblog, NBC News)

**5. Missouri's GOP-drawn map stays benched for November.** The Supreme Court left in place a block on Missouri's new Republican congressional map, drawn to remove Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver's seat. Missouri will use its 2022 lines for the midterms while the underlying fight, including a blocked citizen referendum on the new map, continues in state court. (Bloomberg, NBC News)

**6. Anthropic CEO calls for slower AI development pace.** Dario Amodei, in an interview airing on CBS Sunday Morning today, argued the industry is moving faster than safeguards can keep up with. (CBS News)

## Local and California

**7. Chino Hills sales tax measure heads to November ballot.** The city council placed Measure J on the Nov. 3 ballot, a 1% local sales tax projected to raise about $11.5 million a year for city services and infrastructure, with annual audits and public reporting attached. Debate over the measure, including a dropped ballot argument citing city staff salaries, has been running in the local paper for weeks. (Champion, unrated local)

## Steelman — the other read on the top story

The strongest case against the administration's Iran strategy: NYT reporting on internal US intelligence assessments finds Iran has come out of the strikes more confident, not less, better versed in its own missile, drone, and Hormuz leverage, and betting that America's declining munitions stocks will make a second large-scale strike politically costly. On that read, this week's IAEA referral is a symbolic escalation that both sides know Russia and China will neuter at the Security Council, dressed up as pressure while the actual military and economic contest with Iran drags on with no clear US endgame.
