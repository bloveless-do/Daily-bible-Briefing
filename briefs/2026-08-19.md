# Daily Brief — Wednesday, August 19, 2026

Russia threatens the UK over British-made drones hitting Russian targets, and state propagandists are talking nuclear strikes on Britain. Meta's child-safety trial opened in Oakland with $200 billion on the table. Chino and Chino Hills both put new taxes on the November ballot.

## International

**1. Russia threatens UK "consequences" over drones used in Ukraine's deep strikes.** The Russian Embassy in London says UK-made drones were used in recent long-range Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory, including a fuel depot in Rostov, and called Britain an "accomplice and co-perpetrator." A former Russian deputy foreign minister told the BBC Moscow may now treat the UK as "part of the war." State TV propagandists Vladimir Solovyov and Dmitry Kiselyov have called for nuclear strikes on Britain; UK officials and independent analysts call that talk empty, since the scale needed is far beyond any weapon Russia is known to have. PM Keir Starmer says the rhetoric won't change UK support for Ukraine. (BBC, Reuters)

**2. Ukraine's mid-August drone blitz was one of its largest of the war.** Kyiv launched close to 800 drones and missiles deep into Russia on August 16, hitting Moscow-area and St. Petersburg-area targets and killing at least six people. Ukraine is increasingly using domestically built long-range weapons rather than relying only on Western-supplied systems. (Washington Post, NPR)

## National

**3. Meta's child-safety trial opened in federal court in Oakland.** A coalition of state attorneys general (California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey trying first, 25 more states queued behind them) argues Meta engineered features like infinite scroll, autoplay, and the "like" button specifically to hook minors on Facebook and Instagram, then misled the public about the harm. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers is presiding. States have floated penalties near $200 billion, roughly three years of Meta's after-tax profit. Meta denies the design was deliberate. (Bloomberg, CNBC, NBC News)

> *"Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to sin, it would be better for him to have a great millstone fastened around his neck" (Matt 18:6). That's not a verdict on this specific case. It is the standard the case is being measured against.*

**4. Indiana's flood disaster keeps growing.** Storms beginning August 11 caused historic flooding across central and southern Indiana; at least seven are dead and over 1,000 residents displaced. Gov. Mike Braun declared a statewide emergency and opened a $5,000-per-family relief fund. Trump has directed FEMA toward a presidential disaster declaration, and federal disaster assistance is now flowing to the state. (Indiana Capital Chronicle, ABC News, WFYI)

## Local and California

**5. Chino Hills puts a one-cent sales tax on the November ballot.** The City Council voted unanimously July 14 to place a Transactions and Use Tax measure on the Nov. 3 ballot, projected to raise about $11.5 million a year. Groceries, rent, utilities, education, and prescriptions are exempt. (Champion, unrated local)

**6. Chino puts a business license tax measure on the same ballot.** The City Council voted July 21 to add a business license tax measure to the Nov. 3 ballot, projected to raise about $4.8 million a year. City staff and the police department held a public info session on it Aug. 13. (Champion, unrated local)
